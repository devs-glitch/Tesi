\chapter{The Quantization Study}
\label{ch:quantization}

This chapter describes how the quantized models are built and how they are
compared with their full-precision parents. Every design choice below is either
inherited from the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} or settled by a
measurement reported in Section~\ref{sec:membrane-prep}. Again, the whole analysis is
performed on the validation partition.

\section{Design principle: three separate arms}
\label{sec:grid}

The fundamental design choice is to \textbf{quantize the two targets separately
before quantizing them together}, as separating them turns the grid into a causal ablation and is
what makes the hypothesis of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} testable.
Table~\ref{tab:grid} lists the
configurations that follow; Figure~\ref{fig:arms} shows where in the datapath
each arm acts, and that the two never act on the same tensor.

The \emph{control} arm runs the whole fine-tuning procedure with no quantizer
attached. Both bit-widths are left unset, so neither the weight parametrization
nor the membrane hook is ever registered and the network trains in full
precision. Its purpose is subtractive. The nine quantized configurations differ
from their parent in two respects at once, the bits removed and the operations
that remove them --- reloading the parent, fine-tuning it, selecting a
checkpoint, re-evaluating --- and the control runs those operations alone, so
whatever it produces is their contribution and theirs alone.

It produces two sets of weights. The first is the checkpoint the selection
rule returns, and it is bit-identical
to the parent: the same label on all $1\,435$ validation and $1\,436$ test
images, per-layer firing rates equal to the last digit reported. The identity
follows from the rule: the selection begins with the
un-fine-tuned model already held as a candidate and replaces it only when an
epoch exceeds its validation accuracy \emph{strictly}. With nothing quantized, that initial candidate sits at
the parent's own accuracy, so an epoch would have to beat the parent to displace
it, and on none of the three seeds did it. What the control establishes 
is that reloading the parent, selecting a checkpoint and re-evaluating
leave the model bit-identical, so none of the divergence measured in the other
nine configurations can be charged to the apparatus around the training. The
training itself is not covered by that argument, and it is the subject of the
second set of weights.

The second is the state of the weights after the last epoch, kept separately and
not identical to the parent. Fine-tuning moved them even though it never
succeeded in replacing the checkpoint. What that state measures is the effect of
continued training alone on an explanation, and it is the fine-tuning level of
the calibration (Section~\ref{sec:anchor}).

Two quantities are deliberately left out of the grid. Firstly the membrane decay
$\beta = 0.75$, which is already shift-implementable, having been constrained inside the
hyperparameter search (Section~\ref{sec:constrained-search}) and is not
re-quantized. Secondly, the biases are kept
in full precision: there are 244 of them, under $0.1\,\%$ of the parameters,
and on integer hardware they are added once to a wide accumulator.

The two arms respond differently to the resolution choice. $270\,240$ of the
$295\,332$ parameters are convolutional and therefore resolution-independent, so
what the weight arm saves is nearly constant across resolutions. The membrane
state, which is what the other arm quantizes, falls twelvefold between 224 and
64 pixels.

\section{Quantization-aware fine-tuning from the parent}
\label{sec:qat}

Quantization-aware training is implemented by fake quantization: in the forward
pass each quantized tensor is rounded to its grid and then clamped to the grid's
bounds, and in the backward pass the rounding is replaced by the identity, the
straight-through estimator~\cite{bengio2013ste}.

The two quantizers are attached by
different means because they act on different
kinds of object: a weight is a parameter, owned and updated by the optimizer, while
a membrane potential is a state that is rebuilt at every time step.

Weights are quantized through a \emph{parametrization}. PyTorch moves the
stored tensor aside and recomputes \texttt{weight} from it on every access, so
the module's weight becomes a function of a hidden full-precision tensor.
The optimizer holds and updates that
hidden tensor, the forward pass only ever reads its quantized image, and the
straight-through estimator carries the gradient back to it unchanged. Keeping
the full-precision copy is essential to fine-tuning and makes it possible: an update
smaller than half a quantization step applied to a value already on the grid
would round away and leave nothing behind, whereas if accumulated in the hidden
tensor many such updates eventually carry the weight to the next level.

When the
training ends the parametrization is folded away and the quantized values are
written into ordinary parameters, so the saved checkpoint holds weights
already sitting on the grid.

The membrane is quantized through a \emph{forward hook} on each of the four
hidden LIF layers. A hook that returns a value replaces the output of the
module it is attached to, and a \texttt{Leaky} layer returns the pair of spike
and membrane: the hook passes the spike untouched, binary as it
already is, and substitutes the quantized membrane. That substituted value is
the one the forward loop carries into the following step, so the grid
constrains the state as it crosses a time step and reaches the spikes only by
way of the step after. The membrane grid is fixed in advance, unsigned on $[0, 2]$ from
the clipping study.

\paragraph{Every quantized model is fine-tuned from the full-precision
checkpoint of the same seed.} This is the constraint that guarantees
that the quantization effect is distinct from initialization variance.
The numerical floor of the calibration ($\mathrm{PCC} = 0.975$) holds
for comparisons between a model and its own parent, where no channel permutation
exists, so a quantized model trained from a random initialization would be an
independent solution, for which the relevant level is $0.153$ and almost no
quantization effect would be distinguishable from initialization variance.

\paragraph{Schedule.}
The fine-tuning adapts the parent to its grid. 
Adam runs at one tenth of the reference learning rate
($1.047 \times 10^{-5}$), for at most 20 epochs, with early stopping after 5
epochs without improvement of the validation loss. The reduced learning rate follows from
the constraint above: the floor of the calibration holds only while the
quantized model remains a perturbed copy of its parent, and a step sized for
training from scratch would let it leave that neighbourhood, moving the
comparison toward the independent-solutions level. How far even this schedule
moves a model with nothing quantized is measured in Section~\ref{sec:anchor}.

Before the first epoch the model is evaluated with the quantizer attached and no
training, which gives the \emph{post-training quantization} (PTQ) accuracy of the
same configuration at no extra cost: that un-fine-tuned model is saved as it represents the
first candidate checkpoint, and it is replaced only by an epoch that strictly
improves the validation accuracy. The selected checkpoint is the one on which
every subsequent measurement --- accuracy, explanations, firing statistics,
energy --- is taken. The 30 runs took 13 hours on the same 6\,GB consumer GPU
used for every training run in this thesis.

\paragraph{Gates before the grid.} Two checks were run before any fine-tuning
to validate the quantizer and then to validate the PTQ gate.
The first verifies the quantizer itself: attaching and removing it restores the
original weights exactly, and folding the quantized values into a plain
checkpoint and reloading it leaves no drift ($0$ at 4 bits).

The second targets the
PTQ gate: the joint 8-bit model, with no fine-tuning, must indeed match its parent to
within the spread of the full-precision reference, four samples out of
$1\,435$. At 8 bits the weight step is of the order of the perturbation that
left the spatial correlation at $0.975$ in the calibration, so an appreciable
loss of accuracy there would effectively indicate a defect of the implementation.
Both gates passed (Section~\ref{sec:grid-gates}).

\paragraph{ } All 8-bit configurations were run first, then
4-bit, then 2-bit.

\section{Weight quantizer}
\label{sec:weight-quantizer}

Weights are quantized on a \emph{symmetric signed} grid whose outermost level is
$q_{\max} = 2^{b-1} - 1$ on each side of zero, for a total of $2q_{\max} + 1$:
255 levels at 8 bits, 15 at 4 bits and 3 at 2 bits (the values $-\Delta$, $0$,
$+\Delta$). Zero is exactly representable at every bit-width. The quantizer is
applied to every convolutional and linear weight tensor, including the readout.

\textbf{The scale is computed per output channel.} A single scale for the whole
tensor is set by its largest weight, so a channel whose own weights are small
reaches only the innermost levels of the grid and leaves the external ones unused. The
measured distributions of the reference checkpoint make this effect concrete
(Table~\ref{tab:weight-ranges}): indeed in the deeper convolutions the largest weight
of the weakest channel falls below half the largest weight of the tensor. This
halves the levels that channel can reach and costs it one bit, which at 2 bits it is 
critical: the signed grid is $\{-\Delta, 0, +\Delta\}$ with $\Delta$
the tensor maximum, so every weight of a channel whose maximum lies below
$\Delta/2$ rounds to zero and the channel vanishes. A per-channel scale takes
$\Delta$ from each channel's own maximum, so every channel reaches the
outermost level whatever its magnitude. Figure~\ref{fig:scale-granularity} draws the two
cases for the weakest channel of conv4 at 4 bits: seven of the fifteen levels
are reachable under a scale set by the tensor, fifteen of fifteen under the
channel's own.

$0.0013$ (readout) at 8 bits.

\section{Membrane quantizer}
\label{sec:membrane-quantizer}

The membrane potential of the four hidden LIF layers is quantized at every time
step. The quantizer acts on the potential returned by each layer after the spike
decision, so the quantized value is the \emph{state} carried into the next step:
what is stored, and therefore what costs memory, is what is quantized. The
membrane of the output layer (four neurons) is left in full precision because
its state is negligible in memory and quantizing it would act on the spike
decisions of those four neurons, whose summed counts are the logits; its
\emph{weights} instead are quantized with every other linear tensor
(Section~\ref{sec:weight-quantizer}), so the readout is split between the two
arms.

As has been established, a grid for the membrane can not be designed without knowing the range of the
quantity, and unlike a weight, the potential has no range until the network is
run. Its distribution was therefore measured over the whole validation partition
(Section~\ref{sec:membrane-prep}), and the clipping range was chosen by testing
candidate ranges on the full-precision network, so before any quantization.

The weight scheme is the symmetric per-channel formulation of Jacob et
al.~\cite{jacob2018quantization}. For the membrane no comparable convention
exists: MINT~\cite{yin2024mint} quantizes the potential of a spiking network,
but ties its scale to the weights, which is the coupling
Section~\ref{sec:joint} avoids.

\textbf{The membrane grid is unsigned over $[0, 2]$}, twice the firing threshold
$U_{\text{thr}} = 1$, with $2^b$ levels starting exactly at zero. The step is
$2/(2^b - 1)$:

Zero is the value to which every neuron is initialized and around which it
rests, so it must be exactly representable, which the unsigned grid guarantees
by construction, its first level being zero.

Table~\ref{tab:membrane-grid} contains a quantitative prediction. The step is
$0.8\,\%$ of the threshold at 8 bits, $13.3\,\%$ at 4 bits and $66.7\,\%$ at 2
bits, where it is of the same order as the threshold itself, and where a
neuron's state can encode only whether it is at rest, part-way to threshold, or
above it. Figure~\ref{fig:membrane-grid} draws the three grids on one axis: the
8-bit row reads as a ruler, the 2-bit row as four states. This is the mechanism
behind the observation of Smith et al.~\cite{smith2026} that membrane
quantization needs at least four bits, and the grid tests it directly.

The unsigned grid follows from the clipping study. While potentials do go negative:
the median of \texttt{lif1} sits at $-0.08$ and its first percentile at $-6.6$,
clipping them away changes nothing the classifier uses. The symmetric range
$[-2, +2]$ and the unsigned range $[0, +2]$ return the same accuracy to the last
digit, $0.9826$, one validation sample below the unclipped network, so a
signed grid would spend half of its levels below zero, where no decision is
made. At 8 and 4 bits that costs a factor of two in resolution, one bit. At 2
bits it costs a factor of three: the step grows from $2/3$ to the full $2$, and
no level at all remains between rest and threshold.

\section{Joint arm}
\label{sec:joint}

The joint arm applies both quantizers at once, at the same bit-width, with the
per-channel weight scales of Section~\ref{sec:weight-quantizer} and the fixed
membrane grid of Section~\ref{sec:membrane-quantizer}. \textbf{The two scales are
independent.} MINT~\cite{yin2024mint} shares a single scale between weights and
membrane so that the inference loop needs no rescaling multiplier, but that choice
is an optimization of a specific integer datapath, and imposing it here would
couple the two targets that the grid exists to separate. With independent scales
the joint arm is mechanically the composition of the two single-target arms:
the two quantisers never see each other's grids or state, and that any outcome
of the joint arm that the two single-target arms do not account for is therefore an interaction
within the network, not a coupling introduced by the instrument.
\section{Reading the results}
\label{sec:reading}

For every quantized model, SAM maps are recomputed at the reference layer and
$\gamma$ on twenty validation images per class, and compared with those of
\textbf{the full-precision parent of the same seed}, using the metrics of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics}: spatial PCC, top-20\,\% IoU, the temporal centre of
mass (absolute and signed shift), and the per-timestep correlation curve.

\paragraph{}Further measurements characterize the behaviour:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{cross-faithfulness}: the deletion metric applied to the quantized
        model with its \emph{own} map and with the map of its \emph{parent}, on
        fifteen validation images per class and twenty-one deletion fractions.
        The deletion baseline is
        recomputed for the parent of each seed, and a class is interpretable for
        a seed only if its baseline gap between SAM and random deletion is at
        least $0.05$;
  \item the \textbf{diagnostics} of Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, collected for
        every model in a single pass over the validation partition: per-layer
        firing rate, dead and saturated fractions, CV-ISI, and per-sample
        predictions;
  \item the \textbf{Earth Mover's Distance} between the per-neuron firing-rate
        distributions of quantized model and parent, for a direct comparison with
        Smith et al.~\cite{smith2026}.
\end{itemize}


The CV-ISI follows the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:physical} --- 200
positions per layer, seed 42, a coefficient computed only where at least five
intervals are available.

Five principles, established in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} before the grid was
run, aid in the reading of those numbers.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Below the numerical floor is noise.} A divergence smaller than
        the one produced by weight noise of one 8-bit step ($\mathrm{PCC}$ $0.975$,
        IoU $0.704$, CoM difference $0.029$ steps, worst class) can not be 
        deemed a result. The threshold that decides whether a result belongs
        to the removed bits sits above it, at the fine-tuning level of
        Section~\ref{sec:anchor}.
  \item \textbf{IoU is an early indicator, and PCC a graded measure.} IoU has a
        dynamic range of $2.6\times$ its floor and saturates early, while PCC has
        $34\times$.
  \item \textbf{Everything is reported per class and per seed.} The sensitivity
        ordering Violin\_Mode $>$ Scattered\_Light $>$ Blip $>$ Extremely\_Loud
        recurs under every intervention tested, that is why a pooled figure would average
        a class that responds strongly with one that does not.
  \item \textbf{A flat result on Extremely\_Loud is uninformative.} The
        calibration moved that class by nothing: PCC $0.9997$ under the largest
        perturbation applied, worst per-timestep correlation is $0.996$, a
        sensitivity in $1 - \mathrm{PCC}$ $94$ times below Violin\_Mode's. On
        the other three classes the calibration showed the metrics responding
        to a change known to have been made,but Extremely\_Loud never produced a response
        to calibrate against. This means that a flat reading is consistent both with
        an explanation that did not move and with an instrument that could not
        have seen it move.
  \item \textbf{Cross-faithfulness is interpretable only where the parent has a
        baseline signal.} On the reference seed that means Blip and
        Extremely\_Loud; since the baseline is recomputed per seed, the set of
        interpretable classes is itself reported.
\end{enumerate}

Every quantized model is compared with
\emph{its own} parent, and never with the parent of another seed, which is the
principle that keeps together the other ones and the whole analysis.
Because the three
parents differ from each other far more than any quantized model differs from
its own (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), a comparison against the wrong seed
would produce a large spurious divergence. The guard against that error is
that in every comparison the 8-bit weight arm, the smallest
intervention in the grid, must keep a spatial correlation above $0.98$ with its
parent in every seed.

\paragraph{The test of the hypothesis.} As discussed, the hypothesis predicts an
\emph{ordering}: the membrane arm should displace the temporal centre of mass
beyond its floor at a higher bit-width than the one at which the weight arm
lowers the spatial correlation beyond its floor. The reverse order, or a 
simultaneous change falsifies it.

\section{Stratified analysis}
\label{sec:stratified-method}

Joining the per-sample outputs to the physical descriptors allows the result to
be reported as a relation, which an aggregate divergence does not permit.

The hypothesis is that divergence increases as event strength decreases, with a
mechanism specific to spiking networks: quantizing the membrane potential flips a
neuron's spike decision only where the potential lies within the quantization
error of the threshold, and weak events are exactly what produce potentials that sit close to
threshold. Because a flipped spike is a discrete
event that propagates both to the next layer and forward in time through the
decay and reset, and since SAM is constructed from spike trains, the flip
changes the object the explanation is made of.

The prediction discriminates between arms: membrane quantization applies a fixed
absolute error and should show a steep dependence on event strength, whereas
weight quantization perturbs the input current approximately in proportion to the
signal and should be flatter. It is tested on the membrane arm at 4 and 2 bits,
the configurations in which the arm has an effect and accuracy is intact.

Two preconditions were verified. SNR does translate into image intensity, the
Gravity Spy colour scale being fixed, not auto-scaled per image. SNR is
also very nearly a proxy for class: on the validation partition the top SNR
quartile is $89.7\,\%$ Extremely\_Loud. Stratification uses \textbf{terciles
computed within each class}, fifteen images per class and tercile. The summary
statistic is the IoU gradient, high-SNR tercile minus low-SNR tercile; a
positive gradient means that weak events lose their explanation more.

\section{Energy and memory}
\label{sec:energy-method}

Synaptic operations are counted per layer as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOP}_{\ell} = f_{\ell-1} \cdot T \cdot \mathrm{MAC}_{\ell},
\end{equation}
where $f_{\ell-1}$ is the mean firing rate of the preceding hidden layer and
$\mathrm{MAC}_{\ell}$ the multiply-accumulate count of the equivalent dense
layer, and multiplied by the per-operation costs of~\cite{horowitz2014}. The
rates are the per-hidden-layer figures measured on the \emph{selected checkpoint}
of each configuration, in the same validation pass that produces its
diagnostics, so that energy, accuracy and explanations all refer to the same
model.

\textbf{One layer is asymmetric.} Under direct encoding the first convolution
receives analog pixel values rather than spikes, therefore its operations are
multiply-accumulates. Because the input is identical at every time step, the
current it produces is constant in time and is computed once rather than $T$
times: it is a standard implementation of constant encoding that avoids recomputing 
that layer at every step, which would make the spiking network
more expensive than its convolutional equivalent (Section~\ref{sec:energy-fp32}).

\textbf{Per-operation costs.} Full-precision configurations, the membrane arm
included, use $4.6$\,pJ per MAC and $0.9$\,pJ per accumulate; configurations with
quantized weights use the 8-bit integer figures, $0.2$ and $0.03$\,pJ. Narrower
integer operations are not tabulated, so at 4 and 2 bits the costs are
extrapolated: a multiplication scales quadratically with the operand width and
an accumulate linearly, giving $0.05$ and $0.015$\,pJ at 4 bits and $0.0125$ and
$0.0075$\,pJ at 2 bits. These two rows are estimates.

The first convolution is costed at the weight
bit-width, which assumes that the input image is quantized to the same width;
Section~\ref{sec:energy-grid} shows how much the conclusion depends on that
assumption.

Memory is the number of bits needed to store the $295\,088$ weights plus those
needed to store the membrane state of the $94\,080$ hidden neurons at the
working resolution.
