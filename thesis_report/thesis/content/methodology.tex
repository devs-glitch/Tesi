\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter describes the data pipeline, the model, the procedure by which the
operating configuration was selected, and the implementation of the explainability
metrics. Every measurement reported here is on the validation partition.

\section{Data}

\subsection{Acquisition}

The Gravity Spy machine-learning release~\cite{glanzer2021} is distributed as
eight CSV files, one per detector (H1, L1) and observing run (O1, O2, O3a, O3b).
Each record corresponds to an Omicron trigger with signal-to-noise ratio above
$7.5$ and peak frequency between $10$\,Hz and $2048$\,Hz, and carries the
trigger metadata, the per-class confidence of the Gravity Spy classifier, the
assigned label, and URLs to Omega-scan spectrograms.

\textbf{The labels are the classifications produced by that convolutional
model}, not a physical ground truth and neither the volunteer-combined label of the
separate citizen-science release. For this reason, accuracy reported in this thesis is in agreement
with that reference classifier.

\subsection{Class selection}

The original dataset featured twenty-four classes, of which two are not glitch
morphologies: \texttt{No\_Glitch}, the absence of a morphological trigger, and
\texttt{None\_of\_the\_Above}, the classifier's residual category.
Only four classes were retained --- \textbf{Blip}, \textbf{Extremely\_Loud},
\textbf{Scattered\_Light}, \textbf{Violin\_Mode} --- on three criteria
established before any separability analysis, the successive multivariate checks
are therefore a verification rather than the selection rule.

\emph{Abundance.} These are all sufficiently populated classes, with
$29\,573$, $24\,002$, $99\,665$ and $2\,392$ members respectively. The smallest
sets the balanced cardinality at $2\,392$ per class, sufficient to train without
augmentation (Figure~\ref{fig:label-counts}).

\emph{Morphological diversity.} The four retained classes span
physically distinct families of instrumental noise~\cite{zevin2017, glanzer2023, bahaadini2018}:
short broadband transients of teardrop morphology and $\sim$0.04\,s
duration (Blip)~\cite{cabero2019blip};
high-amplitude events that saturate the spectrogram (Extremely\_Loud);
long-duration arches of 2--2.5\,s correlated with ground motion, caused in O3 by
relative motion between the optical suspension and reaction-mass chains
(Scattered\_Light)~\cite{soni2021scattered}; and narrowband lines at
$\sim$500\,Hz and harmonics, corresponding to resonances of the glass fibres
suspending the mirrors (Violin\_Mode). The choice of spanning the morphological range
serves two purposes: it preserves class separability, which keeps the classification
problem itself manageable, since it is not the main scope of the thesis, and
makes the explanation analysis informative,
since each class should elicit a recognizably different
attention pattern. An example of each is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:examples}.

\emph{Practical constraint.} Four classes keep training and the downstream
quantization grid feasible on a single 6\,GB consumer GPU.

Verification in the tabular domain supported the choice, as it
shows that each class is isolated by a \emph{different}
descriptor, which is the relevant property: Extremely\_Loud occupies
$\log(\text{SNR}+1)$ between 6 and 12 while the others lie between 2 and 5; Blip
is separated by duration; Scattered\_Light sits below roughly 100\,Hz in peak
frequency with a bimodal bandwidth distribution; and Violin\_Mode is the only
class extending to 2\,kHz (Figure~\ref{fig:pairplot}). A UMAP
projection~\cite{mcinnes2018umap} of the standardized descriptors, computed on a
stratified subsample of 500 events per class ($n_\text{neighbors} = 15$,
seed 42), confirms the separation independently (Figure~\ref{fig:umap}).

Violin\_Mode is the weakest case, and this nature along with its
conformation anticipates a
result that recurs throughout the thesis analysis and results:
in the $\log(\text{SNR}+1)$ versus

configuration tested and the most sensitive class in every explainability
measurement.

As a side but dutiful note, restricting the problem to four classes keeps it tractable, but means the results
are not directly comparable to the full Gravity Spy benchmark.

\subsection{Feature selection}

Of the twelve numerical Omicron descriptors, only four were kept and
retained indicative. The feature selection was mainly operated based on four distinct
criteria, direved from their distributions and from their correlation matrix
(Figure~\ref{fig:correlation}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{correlation_matrix.png}
\caption{Pearson correlation between the twelve numerical Omicron descriptors.
The three timestamps are perfectly correlated, and central frequency and
bandwidth correlate at $0.98$.}
\label{fig:correlation}
\end{figure}


\emph{Exact redundancy.} The three timestamp variables (event time, peak time, and start time) are mutually correlated
at $r = 1.00$, being the same GPS time to within sub-second offsets, so at most
one of them could be retained. Central frequency and bandwidth correlate at
$0.98$; bandwidth was retained because peak frequency already encodes spectral
position, so bandwidth supplies extent rather than duplicating location.
 
\emph{Temporal provenance.} Redundancy leaves one timestamp admissible, but none is
retained. A timestamp supplies \emph{when} a glitch occurred rather than
\emph{what} it is, and the correlation matrix shows that the shortcut is available:
event time correlates at $-0.22$ with peak frequency, reflecting instrumental
drift across observing runs. A model given the time of the trigger could
separate the classes by observing period instead of by morphology, and that
separation would not transfer to data recorded later.
 
\emph{Degenerate distributions.} The nanosecond remainders are uniformly
distributed and correlate with everything between $0.00$ and $0.11$, consistent
with timing quantization noise, while the quality factor contains a sentinel value of
order $10^{20}$ that compresses the real distribution to the axis origin.
 
\emph{Reference to the noise floor.} Amplitude and SNR measure the strength of
the same event on two scales. Omicron reports amplitude as a strain amplitude
of the trigger; the SNR is computed on whitened data and is therefore that
amplitude referred to the noise spectral density of the detector at the
frequency and time of the event, $\mathrm{SNR} \sim A / \sqrt{S_n(f,t)}$. The
noise curve spans three to four orders of magnitude between $10$ and
$2048$\,Hz and differs between H1 and L1 and between observing runs, so across
the population the denominator varies more than the numerator. Their linear
correlation is $0.06$, indeed the two descriptors are not statistically redundant,
and neither one is the
substitute for the other. Amplitude was dropped because an absolute strain
amplitude is not comparable across frequency, detector and epoch,
while SNR is comparable and is the quantity on which the release itself is
thresholded, at $7.5$. Amplitude also correlates at $-0.15$ with the three
timestamps against $0.04$ for SNR, carrying the calibration history of the
instrument into the descriptor and reopening a similar shortcut as the one
discussed before.
 
The four retained descriptors --- peak frequency, duration, bandwidth, SNR ---
form a complete and non-redundant characterization of a transient in the
time--frequency plane: spectral position, temporal extent, spectral extent and
prominence. Their largest pairwise correlation is $0.39$, well below the $0.98$
that forced the exclusion of central frequency.
 
Only one of the four image URL columns was kept. The four correspond to
successive time windows of the same detection (0.5\,s, 1\,s, 2\,s, 4\,s), and only the
first was retained. Because the time windows are effectively \emph{different views}, they are not interchangeable,
which is why unavailable images were excluded instead of substituted from another column.
 
Residual class imbalance is removed by random undersampling to the cardinality of
the smallest class, giving an exactly balanced set of $9\,568$ images.
 
\subsection{Preprocessing and partition}
 
The downloaded spectrograms are matplotlib formatted figures comprising a frame with axes,
colour bar and annotation, therefore a centre crop of $470 \times 550$ pixels isolates the
spectrogram panel, which is then resized to the working resolution. The order of operation
matters, since cropping the original and resizing afterwards keeps the same
portion of the spectrogram framed at every resolution. Channel-wise
normalization uses the standard ImageNet statistics, mean
$(0.485, 0.456, 0.406)$ and standard deviation $(0.229, 0.224, 0.225)$. These
are not the statistics of this corpus, and has a consequence that
matters later in the analyses: zero in normalized space is the ImageNet channel mean, and that
is the value the deletion metric substitutes for a removed pixel
(Section~\ref{sec:deletion-method}). A removed pixel is therefore set to a
neutral grey rather than to black, which would itself be a strong input.
 
\textbf{The partition is frozen to disk.} A dedicated script generates the
train/validation/test assignment once, 70\,/\,15\,/\,15 under a fixed seed, and
writes it as a per-file record of path, class and split, so that every subsequent run
reads that file. A file listed but absent from disk is dropped from its partition
with a warning. A file present on disk but absent from the assignment is never
loaded, the loader iterating the assignment rather than the directory, so
no sample can enter a partition without it having been assigned to one.
 
Freezing the partition allows for the assignment to be a function of the file list rather than
of the corpus size, so removing a file removes exactly that file instead of
reshuffling every remaining assignment. And the audit trail is externally
verifiable: a reader can confirm that no file appears in two partitions, and the
manifest records the SHA-256 digest of the assignment file.
 
Four downloaded files proved unreadable. Two were recovered by re-downloading;
two are permanently unavailable at source (0.02\,\% of the corpus) and were
excluded.
 
The final corpus is \textbf{9\,566 images, partitioned $6\,695 / 1\,435 /
1\,436$}.  Beyond class balance, the partitions are matched on the physical
properties of the events: the medians of SNR, duration, peak frequency and
bandwidth are $14.97 / 14.09 / 14.98$, $1.25 / 1.25 / 1.10$\,s,
$75.3 / 75.3 / 75.3$\,Hz and $4112 / 4254 / 4131$\,Hz.
\section{Model}
 
\subsection{Architecture}
 
\texttt{GWGlitchSNN} is a four-block convolutional spiking network. Each block is
a $5 \times 5$ convolution with stride 2 and padding 2 followed by a LIF layer. The
spatial downsampling is performed by the stride rather than by pooling, in this way
the spike tensors are kept dense in time and it avoids inserting a non-spiking operator
between layers.

The forward pass returns the stacked output spike train \emph{and} the four
hidden spike tensors of shape $[T, B, C, H, W]$, so that every diagnostic and
explainability procedure consumes raw hidden spike trains directly from the model
interface.
 
Parameter count is nearly resolution-independent: $295\,332$ at 112\,px, of which
$270\,240$ are convolutional and unaffected by input size, since only the linear
readout shrinks with resolution, a fact with consequences for the design of the
quantization grid discussed in Section~\ref{sec:grid}.
 
Batch normalization, pooling and dropout are intentionally absent. Normalization
layers interact non-trivially with quantization, and their treatment belongs to a
later phase. Batch normalization must indeed be reconsidered for spiking networks in
general, as quantizing its parameters is a problem distinct from quantizing weights
and potentials. In this persepctive, two alternative hardware strategies exist --- approximating
it with shifts, or eliminating it entirely.
 
\subsection{Readout and loss}
 
Classification uses a \emph{rate-coded readout}: output spikes are summed over
the $T$ time steps and the resulting per-class counts are passed to a
cross-entropy loss as logits. Optimization uses Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}. This
keeps SAM in the multi-spike regime, where its inter-spike-interval formulation
remains meaningful.
 
\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}
 
\subsection{Hyperparameter optimization}
 
Hyperparameters are searched with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna}, maximizing
validation accuracy. Each trial reports validation accuracy after every epoch
and is pruned by a Hyperband pruner~\cite{li2018hyperband} (minimum resource 1
epoch, maximum 15, reduction factor 3), which allocates epochs to promising
configurations and truncating the others early. Studies are persisted to SQLite,
one study name per search and resolution.
 
Two studies were run. The first (v4), at 224\,px resolution, searched the learning rate in
the interval $[10^{-4}, 3 \times 10^{-3}]$ (log-uniform), $\beta$ in $(0, 1)$ and
$T \in \{5, \dots, 15\}$, and selected a learning rate of
$1.036 \times 10^{-4}$, $\beta = 0.4148$ and $T = 7$. This was the configuration
used for the resolution study. The second (v5), at the working resolution found
after the study (112\,px, as it will be better discussed in Section~\ref{sec:Selecting the operating configuration}),
imposed the deployment constraints described in
Section~\ref{sec:constrained-search} and produced the configuration used
throughout the rest of the thesis.
 
\subsection{Experiment tracking}
 
Every trial is logged to Weights \& Biases. Beyond validation loss and
accuracy, the logged metrics also include the mean firing rate of each of the four
hidden layers. Firing rate is the proxy for energy consumption in spiking
hardware~\cite{roy2019towards}, so tracking it per layer during the search shows
whether high-accuracy configurations achieve their accuracy through dense and
expensive activity or through sparse spiking, and also whether any layer is silent or
saturated before any explainability diagnostic is run.
 
A separately logged quantity, recorded under the name
\texttt{avg\_firing\_rate}, is the mean over the four \emph{output} neurons only.
A value near $0.25$ indicates that roughly one output neuron fires consistently,
which is a signature of confident classification.
It is relevant to note that it is \emph{not} the global firing rate of the network and is
never used for energy accounting, for which only the per-hidden-layer rates are
valid.
 
\subsection{Final training}
 
The training script reads the frozen configuration and trains the model. The script is
parametrized on input resolution and seed, the same code indeed serves the
resolution study, the final training and the multi-seed reference. The seed is
set explicitly before the model is constructed. The checkpoint is written
whenever validation accuracy improves, and training stops after five
consecutive epochs without improvement in validation loss, which permits early stopping,
and with a ceiling set at 50 epochs.
Each run writes the checkpoint together with a record of the full
per-epoch history, the per-layer firing rates, the per-class validation recall,
the seed and the resolution.
 
\section{Selecting the operating configuration}
\label{sec:Selecting the operating configuration}
 
The final operating point is defined by the input resolution, the three
hyperparameters, and the layer and decay constant at which the explanations
are read. Each is settled on evidence measured on the validation partition and
recorded in a single frozen manifest.
 
\subsection{Input resolution}
 
Nine training runs were performed: three resolutions (224, 112, 64\,px) at three
seeds each, on the frozen partition, with hyperparameters held fixed and only the
input size varying. Since those hyperparameters had been found at 224\,px, the
protocol systematically handicaps the lower resolutions, so any deficit they
show is an upper bound on the deficit attributable to resolution itself.
 
The procedure and its outcome are reported in Section~\ref{sec:resolution}.
 
\subsection{Hyperparameter search under deployment constraints}
\label{sec:constrained-search}
 
The search uses Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna} with a Hyperband
pruner~\cite{li2018hyperband}, maximizing validation accuracy over three
hyperparameters chosen because they govern three distinct axes: learning rate
(optimization stability), $\beta$ (membrane leak and temporal memory), and $T$
(latency, computation and memory cost).
 
\textbf{Two deployment constraints are imposed inside the search space.} The
first restricts $\beta$ to $\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.875\}$, values whose decay is
realizable as a bit shift, the set spanning both families ($\beta = 2^{-k}$ and
$\beta = 1 - 2^{-k}$). The second caps $T$ at 8. Constraining the search
guarantees that whatever it returns is deployable by construction, and
Section~\ref{sec:hyperparameters} shows that this is not equal to rounding an
unconstrained optimum afterwards: the free search selects $\beta = 0.4148$,
whose admissible neighbours are $0.25$ and $0.5$, while the constrained search
selects $0.75$.
 
The lower bound of the learning rate was moved from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$,
because the v4 optimum ($1.036 \times 10^{-4}$) sat on the boundary of the
earlier range, and a boundary optimum should be investigated further to clarify whether it is
a consequence of the decided range.
 
\subsection{Baseline manifest}
 
The outcome of these choices is recorded in a single file,
\texttt{baseline\_manifest.json}. It contains the input resolution, the three
hyperparameters, the originating study, the decay rate and reference layer of
the explainer, the list of seeds (1234, 2345, 3456), the checkpoint paths, the
size of each partition, and the first sixteen hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256
digest of the partition file (\texttt{754b2515b6509075}).
Every downstream script reads the model and its configuration from the manifest.

 
\section{Diagnostics of the network dynamics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}
 
Two diagnostics characterize whether a trained network is a healthy spiking
system or a formally correct one whose neurons are dead or saturated. The two are
reused unchanged on every quantized model.
 
\paragraph{Dead and saturated neurons.} For each hidden layer, the mean firing rate of every individual neuron is
accumulated across the validation partition, weighted by batch size. A rate
below $0.01$ identifies a \emph{dead} neuron, while a rate above $0.80$ a \emph{saturated}
one. Neither cutoff is derived, since thresholds of this kind are conventions read
off the distribution: the pruning literature trims neurons whose fraction of
zero activations exceeds one standard deviation of the layer
mean~\cite{hu2016trimming}, and the dormant-neuron analysis treats the cutoff
as a tuned hyperparameter, reporting values between $0$ and
$0.1$~\cite{sokar2023dormant}.

The two ends are pathological for different reasons. A neuron firing on fewer
than one input in a hundred is wasted capacity and, being silent, it cannot
appear in any explanation; its output is near-deterministic, carrying $0.081$
of the one bit a binary unit can hold. Saturation, instead, is not an information
problem --- a Bernoulli variable at $0.80$ retains $0.722$ bits so as much as one
at $0.20$ --- but an energy one: the energy of a spiking layer scales with the
number of spikes it emits~\cite{suetake2023penalty}, so a unit near its ceiling
spends the sparsity the architecture was chosen for.

\paragraph{Regularity of firing.} The coefficient of variation of the
inter-spike intervals,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CV} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{ISI}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{ISI}}},
\end{equation}
distinguishes regular, clock-like firing ($\mathrm{CV} \to 0$) from irregular,
Poisson-like firing ($\mathrm{CV} \approx 1$). It is relevant here because the
Spike Activation Map (SAM) is exactly built on inter-spike intervals. A random sample of 200
neuron positions per layer is fixed once at the first batch, and the intervals
of exactly those positions are accumulated over every validation image. The CV
is then computed only where at least five intervals were observed. Since a neuron can
produce at most $T - 1 = 7$ intervals within a single image, pooling over the
whole partition is what makes the estimate stable.
 
\section{The Spike Activation Map}
\label{sec:sam-method}
 
The definition is the one given in Section~\ref{sec:sam-background}: the
recency weight of Equation~\ref{eq:tscs}, accumulated over a neuron's preceding
spikes by Equation~\ref{eq:ncs}, masked by the spikes emitted at $t$ and summed
over channels by Equation~\ref{eq:sam}. What the definition leaves open the
implementation had to settle.

\paragraph{The summation range is exclusive of $t$.} The score measures the contribution of a neuron's
\emph{preceding} trajectory, as including $t' = t$ would mean adding a term of weight
$\exp(0) = 1$ for every neuron firing at $t$, so the map produced is the correct
SAM \emph{plus the raw binary spike map}. The pedestal is independent of both
$\gamma$ and the spike history, and therefore inflates every correlation between
maps computed at different $\gamma$. A consequence of the correct definition is
that SAM at $t = 0$ is identically zero, no past history existing at the first
step.
 
The implementation uses the recursion
\begin{equation}
N_{t+1} = e^{-\gamma}\left(N_{t} + S_{t}\right), \qquad N_0 = 0,
\end{equation}
algebraically identical to Equation~\ref{eq:ncs} but $O(T)$ rather than
$O(T^2)$. A reference implementation computing the explicit sum is retained and
the two are asserted equal at run time.
 
Visualizations overlay every time step on the denormalized input under a
\emph{shared colour scale} to assure that a change in intensity across time is genuine
and is not an artefact of per-panel rescaling.
 
The reference configuration for every analysis below is \textbf{layer 2 with
$\gamma = 0.5$}, both recorded in the manifest. The choice of $\gamma$ is
justified in Section~\ref{sec:gamma}.
 
\section{Evaluating and comparing explanations}
\label{sec:xai-protocol}
 
\subsection{Multi-sample statistics}
 
For each class, SAM is computed on 25 validation images, aggregated
over time (mean over $T$) and stacked, and three maps are reported: the
\emph{mean}, meaning the region attended on average; the \emph{standard deviation},
showing where the explanation varies most across
instances; and their ratio, the
\emph{coefficient of variation}, which indicates whether a region is attended
consistently or only in some examples. Mean and standard deviation share a
colour scale across classes so that they are numerically comparable. The
coefficient of variation has an extremely long tail where the mean is near zero,
and its colour scale is truncated at the 95th percentile.

The same statistics are repeated for all four hidden layers, on 20 images per
class. Since SAM sums over the channel axis and the four layers have 16, 32, 64
and 128 channels, raw magnitudes are not comparable across depth, and both maps
are normalized by the channel count of their layer before being plotted on a
shared scale.
 
\subsection{Faithfulness: the deletion metric}

Stability does not by itself imply correctness, so faithfulness is tested
separately with the
deletion metric of Petsiuk et al.~\cite{petsiuk2018rise}. Pixels are removed in
decreasing order of attributed importance and the confidence of the model is
read after each removal. A map that points at the evidence the model uses
destroys that confidence faster than one that does not.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:deletion} states the procedure for a single image;
\texttt{xai/deletion\_metric.py} implements it.
 
\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Deletion metric for one image}
\label{alg:deletion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE network $f$, image $x$, reference layer $\ell$, decay $\gamma$,
         fractions $F = \{0,\, 0.05,\, \dots,\, 1\}$
\ENSURE  confidence curves $c^{\mathrm{SAM}}$ and $c^{\mathrm{rand}}$ over $F$
\STATE $M \gets \mathrm{SAM}(f, x, \ell, \gamma)$ \COMMENT{$[T, H, W]$, one map per time step}
\STATE $A \gets \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t} M_{t}$
\STATE $A \gets \mathrm{upsample}_{\mathrm{bilinear}}(A)$ \COMMENT{layer resolution to input resolution}
\STATE $\hat{y} \gets \arg\max_{c} \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(f(x)\right)_{c}$
\FOR{$\mathrm{ord} \in \{\mathrm{SAM},\, \mathrm{rand}\}$}
  \IF{$\mathrm{ord} = \mathrm{SAM}$}
    \STATE $\pi \gets \mathrm{argsort}(A,\ \text{descending})$
  \ELSE
    \STATE $\pi \gets$ uniform random permutation of $\{1, \dots, HW\}$
  \ENDIF
  \FOR{$\phi \in F$}
    \STATE $x' \gets x$ with pixels $\pi_{1 \dots \lfloor \phi HW \rfloor}$ set to $0$ in all three channels
    \STATE $c^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\phi} \gets \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(f(x')\right)_{\hat{y}}$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
 
SAM produces one map per time
step and the metric needs one, so the maps are averaged over $t$, and what survives
that average is an ordering of the pixels: only the ordering enters what
follows. SAM is class-agnostic, so the tracked class is the one the model
predicts on the unmodified image, held fixed while the pixels disappear.
Removed pixels are set to zero in normalized space, which is the ImageNet
channel mean of the preprocessing rather than black.
 
The pixel ranking is computed once per image and per ordering, and the mask at
each fraction takes its first $\lfloor \phi H W \rfloor$ entries. The removed
sets are nested: a pixel deleted at one fraction stays deleted at every larger
one. What the curve traces is the progressive degradation of a single ranking,
not 21 unrelated maskings.

At every fraction the two curves delete the same number of pixels and differ
only in which ones. The gap between them isolates the ordering, the one thing
the control changes, and cannot come from one condition removing more of the
image than the other.

The area under a curve is the confidence the model retains, integrated over the
fraction deleted: a faithful map destroys that confidence early and leaves less
area behind than random deletion, which makes
$\Delta = \mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{SAM}} - \mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{random}}$ negative,
its magnitude the margin the map gains over chance. The 15 curves of a class are
averaged point by point, and the area under the mean curve is taken by the
trapezoidal rule, which joins consecutive samples by a straight segment and sums
the 20 trapezia lying between the 21 fractions. The curve has no analytic form
and only 21 empirical samples, so a higher-order rule would interpolate
structure that was never measured.

\subsection{Divergence metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

Two explanations of the same image produced by two models are compared with
different metrics addressing different questions.
The first two read the stack summed over time,
$A = \sum_{t} M_{\cdot\,\cdot,\,t}$, and are spatial, while the third reads the stack
unaggregated and is the only one that sees the time axis. Their provenance in
the saliency literature is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sam-background}.

\paragraph{Spatial Pearson correlation (PCC).} The linear correlation between
the two summed maps, flattened over the $HW$ positions,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PCC}(A, B) =
\frac{\sum_{i} (A_i - \bar{A})(B_i - \bar{B})}
     {\sqrt{\sum_{i} (A_i - \bar{A})^2}\ \sqrt{\sum_{i} (B_i - \bar{B})^2}} .
\label{eq:pcc}
\end{equation}
Centring and dividing by the standard deviations makes it invariant to any
affine rescaling $B \mapsto \alpha B + \beta$ with $\alpha > 0$, and that
invariance is what allows a comparison across models whose overall spiking
activity differs. Indeed, a quantized model that fires uniformly more than its parent
is not charged for the offset, only for a change in the spatial pattern. The
quantity is undefined when either map is constant, a case that in the implementation
returns as missing rather than as zero.

\paragraph{Top-$k$ intersection over union (IoU).} Let $A_k$ hold the positions
whose value in $A$ exceeds the $(1-k)$ empirical quantile of $A$, and $B_k$ the
same for $B$. The metric is the Jaccard index of the two sets,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IoU}_k = \frac{|A_k \cap B_k|}{|A_k \cup B_k|},
\label{eq:iou}
\end{equation}
with $k = 0.20$, the value used by Rabbi et al.~\cite{rabbi2026}. Where the
correlation weighs every position by its value, this reads the ranking alone
and only at its top, as it asks whether the two models highlight the same region,
not whether they assign it the same intensity. Two independent rankings select
$kHW$ positions each and share $k^{2}HW$ of them in expectation, over a union
of $2kHW - k^{2}HW$, so the chance level is $k/(2-k) = 0.111$.

The threshold is
a strict inequality against an empirical quantile, so the selected set holds
exactly $kHW$ positions only when the values are distinct. Ties can leave it
empty, which happens when $k$ is small enough for the quantile to fall inside a
plateau, and the value is then undefined.

\paragraph{Temporal centre of mass (CoM).} The mean time step at which the
energy of the SAM stack is concentrated,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CoM} = \frac{\sum_t t \, E_t}{\sum_t E_t},
\qquad E_t = \sum_{i,j} M_{ij,t},
\label{eq:com}
\end{equation}
with the steps indexed from $0$ and $E_0 = 0$, SAM being identically zero at
the first step. It is reported both as a signed difference between the two
centres of mass, whose sign says whether the energy of the explanation moves
earlier or later, and as its absolute value, which is the form the numerical
floors are calibrated against. Both are in units of time steps. Aggregating over
the whole map discards where the activity sits and keeps when it occurs, which
is what makes it the complement of the two spatial metrics.

Beyond the three, the \emph{per-timestep correlation} applies
Equation~\ref{eq:pcc} to the pair of maps at each $t$ separately and returns a
vector of length $T$ in place of a scalar. It localizes the step at which
agreement breaks down, which provides an information that neither
a summed map nor a single centre of mass can show.
 
\subsection{Calibration protocol}
\label{sec:calibration-protocol}
 
The divergence metrics are calibrated before they are used, at different levels,
all on fifteen validation images per class at the reference layer and $\gamma$,
on the three checkpoints of the full-precision reference.
 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Verification.} A checkpoint compared with itself must return
        perfect agreement, so two calls to the sampling routine must return
        bit-identical images, and two forward passes of the same model must
        return identical maps.
  \item \textbf{Independent solutions.} The three full-precision checkpoints are
        compared pairwise to measure how similar two independently trained
        solutions are.
  \item \textbf{Numerical floor.} Gaussian noise is added to the weight tensors
        of one checkpoint, at magnitudes expressed as fractions of its measured
        8-bit quantization step, and the perturbed model is then compared with the
        unperturbed one. Biases and the LIF buffers are excluded, since
        perturbing $\beta$ would mix membrane dynamics with weight noise.
\end{enumerate}
The $\gamma$ sensitivity analysis supplies a further reference point: the
divergence produced by changing the explainer itself thirtyfold.
