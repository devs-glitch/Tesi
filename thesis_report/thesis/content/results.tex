The ordering in Table~\ref{tab:resolution-accuracy} is not monotonic, as 64\,px
outperforms 112\,px, and a genuine resolution effect would produce monotonic
degradation. $0.9854$ corresponds to $21.0$ errors out of
$1\,435$ validation samples and $0.9814$ to $26.7$, a gap of $5.7$ samples,
against a 95\,\% Wilson interval on a single measurement of about $0.7$
percentage points. The four classes survive a twelvefold reduction in pixel
count, which further confirm their being distinguished by coarse morphology.

Per-class recall is directional but small. Violin\_Mode degrades most from
224\,px to 64\,px, by $1.00$ percentage points, which is the direction predicted
from morphology since narrow spectral lines are the structure most vulnerable to
downsampling --- but the magnitude is $3.7$ samples out of $365$, inside the
confidence interval of a single measurement. Extremely\_Loud degrades more at
112\,px than at 64\,px, a second non-monotonicity. The single most 
relevant observation is that \textbf{no class collapses at
reduced resolution.}

\subsection{Network health does discriminate}

Since accuracy is uninformative, the resolution was decided on the internal state
of the network. For each hidden layer, the mean firing rate of every individual
neuron is accumulated across the validation partition: a rate below $0.01$
identifies a \emph{dead} neuron, above $0.80$ a \emph{saturated} one.

The two ends are pathological for different reasons. A neuron firing on fewer
than one input in a hundred is wasted capacity and, being silent, cannot appear
in any explanation; its output is near-deterministic, carrying $0.081$ of the
one bit a binary unit can hold. Saturation is not an information problem itself --- a
Bernoulli variable at $0.80$ retains $0.722$ bits, as much as one at $0.20$ ---
but an energy one: the energy of a spiking layer scales with the number of
spikes it emits~\cite{suetake2023penalty}, so a unit near its ceiling
is essentially spending the
sparsity the architecture was chosen for.

Neither cutoff level (dead and saturated) is derived.
Thresholds of this kind are conventions elsewhere
too: the pruning literature trims neurons whose fraction of zero activations
exceeds one standard deviation of the layer mean~\cite{hu2016trimming}, and the
dormant-neuron analysis treats the cutoff as a tuned hyperparameter, reporting
values between $0$ and $0.1$~\cite{sokar2023dormant}. Sweeping both cutoffs on
the adopted configuration shows no plateau (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}). A factor of
two either side of $0.01$ moves the dead fraction by $11.2$, $9.0$, $6.7$ and
$11.2$ percentage points in the four layers, so the distribution of rates holds
no gap separating dead units from live ones. The dead fraction is the value of a
monotone function read at a conventional point, 
and it carries meaning only as a comparison at fixed
cutoff, which is how Table~\ref{tab:dead} uses it.

Layer 4 carries the resolution effect. The dead fraction falls monotonically 
from $36.3$\,\% to $19.5$\,\%, while
the median per-neuron firing rate rises from $0.0399$ to $0.1499$. These were the
two competing explanations for the higher mean activity observed at lower
resolution --- more neurons participating, or the same neurons firing harder ---
and both occur. Layer 2 also falls monotonically, by $5.4$ percentage points
against layer 4's $16.8$; layers 1 and 3 are not monotonic at all. Saturation instead 
does not increase to compensate: neurons above $0.80$ remain below $2.5$\,\% in
layer 4 at every resolution.

\textbf{The interpretation of this effect is attributed to spatial redundancy.} 
Layer 4
contains $25\,088$ neurons at 224\,px and $6\,272$ at 112\,px, so in absolute
terms the number of \emph{live} neurons falls from roughly $15\,970$ to $4\,789$.
Reducing the resolution does not increase usable capacity: it reduces capacity
while preferentially discarding units that were contributing nothing. At 224\,px
the deepest layer views a $14 \times 14$ map derived from a largely empty
spectrogram, and a third of its units never encounter signal.

\subsection{Decision: 112 pixels}

Most of the benefit comes from the first halving: 224 to 112\,px recovers $12.7$
percentage points of dead fraction, the further move to 64\,px only recovers $4.1$ more.
Membrane state at 8 bits falls from $368$ to $92$\,KiB --- the state of all four
hidden layers held at once, which is the part of the footprint that must sit in
fast memory and be rewritten at every one of the $T$ steps, the weights being
read-only.

The decisive argument against 64\,px is the spatial resolution of the
explanations themselves. SAM maps are computed on layer activations, whose side
lengths are 56, 28, 14 and 7 at 112\,px but 32, 16, 8 and 4 at 64\,px. A
top-20\,\% overlap needs enough positions for its selected set to describe a
region: at 112\,px the second layer offers $784$ positions, a selected set of
about $157$, and the third $196$; at 64\,px the second layer offers $256$, a set
of about $51$, and the third $64$, a set of about thirteen. The temporal centre of mass sums
over positions and is unaffected; the spatial metrics are not. Since the
contribution of this thesis is the fidelity of explanations, the resolution that
keeps a usable spatial map deeper in the network is preferred, whatever the
lower one saves in compute.

\section{Hyperparameters and the full-precision reference}
\label{sec:hyperparameters}

The constrained search selected \textbf{$\beta = 0.75$, $T = 8$, learning rate
$1.047 \times 10^{-4}$} at a best validation accuracy of $0.9847$ within the
15-epoch budget.

\textbf{The constraint on $\beta$ is not equivalent to rounding.} The
unconstrained search, run at 224\,px, selects $\beta = 0.4148$, whose admissible
neighbours are $0.25$ and $0.5$. The constrained search at the working resolution
selects $0.75$, a value that rounding would never have reached. Rounding the
unconstrained optimum after the fact would have produced $0.5$, a value the
constrained search had available and did not select.

\textbf{$T = 8$ is an optimum at the imposed ceiling}:
the search was forbidden from exploring $T > 8$. The mitigating evidence is that
an unconstrained search with range up to 15 selected $T = 7$, so the preference
for long integration windows is mild.

\subsection{The operating point improves the measurement instrument}

Holding resolution and partition fixed and moving from the unconstrained
configuration ($\beta = 0.4148$, $T = 7$) to the adopted one ($\beta = 0.75$,
$T = 8$), the layer-4 dead fraction is \emph{unchanged} at $23.6\,\%$, while
layers 1 to 3 lose between $1.5$ and $6.3$ percentage points ($17.8 / 11.8 /
13.0$ to $11.5 / 10.3 / 11.5\,\%$, three seeds each). The change of
configuration, $\beta$ and $T$ together, leaves the deep-layer deficit where it
was, and resolution moves it. This supports the reading of
Section~\ref{sec:resolution}: the deep-layer problem is spatial redundancy, not a
dynamical pathology curable by a better decay constant.

What the change affects is saturation, by an order of magnitude and most
where it matters. Neurons above $0.80$ fall from $9.3 / 3.5 / 2.7 / 1.8$\,\%
across the four layers to $4.7 / 0.22 / 0.06 / 0.14$\,\%. The layer-2 figure 
(where the SAM map is computed) is
the consequential one. A saturated neuron fires at nearly
every step. It is then almost always firing at $t$, and always arrives there
with a long spike history, so it accumulates the maximum
possible contribution score on every image and therefore contributes a constant,
non-discriminative floor to every SAM map. \textbf{Selecting on validation
accuracy alone happened to reduce that contamination by a factor of sixteen at
the reference layer.}

\textbf{Accuracy is reproducible, while the internal dynamics are not.} At fixed accuracy
the mean firing rate of layer 1 varies by a factor of $2.6$ across seeds, and the
coefficient of variation falls monotonically with depth: $46.9$\,\% at layer 1,
$28.6$\,\% at layer 2, $15.3$\,\% at layer 3, $9.5$\,\% at layer 4. Furthermore, this regime is
established within the first epoch and persists, which indicates that the initialization
selects the firing regime and training does not subsequently equalize it. In the units used for the grid comparison of Section~\ref{sec:disagreement}, the
Wasserstein-1 distance between the per-neuron firing-rate distributions of two
full-precision models differing only in seed falls with depth: $0.160$, $0.066$,
$0.036$ and $0.022$ from layer 1 to layer 4, averaged over the three pairs, with
the pairs spanning $0.088$--$0.240$ in layer 1 and $0.018$--$0.024$ in layer 4.
Section~\ref{sec:disagreement} reads every quantized model against those
figures, layer by layer. This is the observation that
motivates Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.

\section{Fixing the explainer}

\subsection{Choice of the decay rate}
\label{sec:gamma}

SAM has one free parameter, $\gamma$, which represents 
\textbf{the decay of a temporal kernel, so it must be evaluated on
the temporal axis.} Comparing maps averaged over time removes precisely the axis
on which $\gamma$ acts, and a high spatial correlation is then close to
guaranteed regardless of $\gamma$. Three quantities are therefore measured: the
spatial Pearson correlation between time-aggregated maps, retained for continuity
with the literature; the \emph{temporal centre of mass} of the SAM energy; and
the \emph{per-timestep correlation} against a reference $\gamma$.

These metrics were checked analytically:
on a fully firing spike train with $T = 8$ the centre of mass moves from $4.86$
at $\gamma = 0.1$ to $4.02$ at $\gamma = 3.0$, so the metric does respond. A
second check comes free from the definition. At $t = 1$ the score is
$e^{-\gamma} S_0$, so changing $\gamma$ multiplies the map by a constant, and
Pearson correlation is affine-invariant. The measured per-timestep correlation at
$t = 1$ is exactly $1.000$ for every $\gamma$.

Two main conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:gamma}. 
\textbf{The temporal shift is kernel arithmetic, and not explanation structure.} The
measured shift of $0.83$--$0.93$ steps is what a \emph{fully firing} spike train
produces, $0.84$. It is also what a random train produces at any density: $0.84$
at a firing rate of $0.05$, $0.83$ at $0.19$, the rate measured in layer 2, and
$0.84$ at $0.70$. The independence is exact because with
Bernoulli trains at rate $p$ the expected SAM energy at step $t$ is
$p^{2}$ times a function of $\gamma$ alone, and the $p^{2}$ cancels in the ratio
that defines the centre of mass. Changing $\gamma$ therefore moves the centre of
mass by an amount that carries no information about which neurons fired or when.
For this reason the centre of mass against $\gamma$ is \emph{not} a criterion for
selecting $\gamma$ --- though it remains valid as a divergence metric, where
models are compared at fixed $\gamma$ and the trivial component cancels.

\textbf{Sensitivity is strongly class-dependent.} In terms of $1 - \mathrm{PCC}$,
Violin\_Mode is 57 times more sensitive than Extremely\_Loud. The ordering
Violin\_Mode $>$ Scattered\_Light $>$ Blip $>$ Extremely\_Loud recurs under the
weight perturbation of Section~\ref{sec:perturbation}.

Within $\gamma \in [0.3, 0.9]$ all per-timestep correlations remain above $0.98$
even in the worst class, and only $\gamma = 3.0$ diverges appreciably.
\textbf{$\gamma = 0.5$ is adopted}, the value of the original
formulation~\cite{kim2021sam}, which is also supported by evidence on the axis $\gamma$
 governs (Figure~\ref{fig:gamma}).

\subsection{Faithfulness}
\label{sec:faithfulness}

Robustness to $\gamma$ is necessary but not sufficient, since a stable explanation could
still be stably wrong. The deletion metric~\cite{petsiuk2018rise} removes pixels in
decreasing order of SAM score and records the confidence assigned to the class
predicted on the unmodified image, and comparing against the removal of pixel in random order.
 
Deleted pixels are set to zero \emph{in normalized space}, the ImageNet channel
mean of the preprocessing, a neutral grey rather than black.
The same map
is used for both orderings, and the ordering is computed once per image and
sliced progressively, so the masked sets are nested: what is removed at 50\,\%
stays removed at 55\,\%.

The metric separates SAM from the baseline on two classes and not on the other
two, and the split is systematic (Table~\ref{tab:deletion},
Figure~\ref{fig:deletion}). Blip and Extremely\_Loud show reductions of $35$ and
$32$\,\% in area. On Scattered\_Light and Violin\_Mode deleting the
pixels SAM ranks highest destroys confidence \emph{more slowly} than deleting
pixels at random, by $10.1$ and $5.4$\,\% of area, which is roughly a third and a
sixth of the $35$ and $32$\,\% by which it runs faster on the other two classes.
Fifteen images per class and no interval on the areas do not support a stronger
reading: on these two classes SAM is not shown to be faithful, which is not the
same as being shown to point away from the evidence.

The pattern follows again the spatial geometry of the classes. 
Blip is a compact vertical line and Extremely\_Loud a dominant saturated
structure, so removing the high-scoring region removes the defining evidence.
On the other hand
Scattered\_Light is an extended horizontal band, and Violin\_Mode a set of thin
distributed lines: the interpretation is that random deletion breaks 
the pattern across the whole image,
whereas SAM-guided deletion concentrates removal in one region and leaves the
rest --- which still carries sufficient evidence --- intact.

The random baseline of Extremely\_Loud, $0.8936$, is the highest of the four and
is independent confirmation of that class's behaviour that its evidence is
distributed across the whole image.

\section{Calibrating the divergence metrics}
\label{sec:calibration}

Since the metrics used to compare two explanations return numbers between zero and one
whatever is fed to them. Three questions must be answered before any quantization
result can be interpreted: what they report when nothing has changed, when
something negligible has changed, and when everything has changed.

All measurements use fifteen validation images per class at layer 2 with
$\gamma = 0.5$, on the three checkpoints of Table~\ref{tab:fp32}.

\subsection{Verification of the instrument}

The verification comes from the control arm, whose selected checkpoint is
bit-identical to its parent (Section~\ref{sec:grid-gates}): compared against that
parent the metrics return $\mathrm{PCC} = 1$, $\mathrm{IoU} = 1$ and a
centre-of-mass difference of $0$, in every class and every seed. This means that two models that
are the same model therefore produce the same explanation under the full
comparison pipeline, image sampling included, which is what the measurement
assumes. The network is evaluated with gradients disabled, the images are the
first of each class in the fixed order of the validation loader, identical for
every model, and under direct encoding the forward pass involves no sampling, so
the cross-model differences below cannot be run-to-run noise.

\textbf{The spatial metrics collapse.} Two models agreeing to within one
validation sample in accuracy produce maps correlating at $0.153$ on
Scattered\_Light, with a top-20\,\% overlap of $0.234$ against a chance level of
$0.111$. For comparison, a thirtyfold change in $\gamma$ reduces the correlation
only to $0.960$: so initialization variance is 21 times larger than that effect.

The collapse is also not uniform across pairs. On Blip the three seed pairs
correlate at $0.34$, $0.72$ and $0.78$, so which two networks are compared
matters more than which class is compared.

The collapse is not a relabelling of channels. SAM sums the contribution scores
over the channel axis (Equation~\ref{eq:sam}), so two networks whose layer-2
channels were the same features in a different order would produce identical
maps. Positions, unlike channels, cannot be permuted: they are tied to the input
image. A low correlation between seeds therefore means that the two networks
\textbf{concentrate their layer-2 activity on different regions of the same
spectrogram}. Three models of equal accuracy are not three copies of one
solution: the firing rate of their first layer differs by a factor of $2.6$
(Section~\ref{sec:hyperparameters}), and their spatial explanations differ as
well. The class ordering of Section~\ref{sec:gamma} does not survive the change of
seed either: across seeds the most disturbed class is Scattered\_Light, ahead of
Blip and then Violin\_Mode, whereas within a fixed network Violin\_Mode is always
the most sensitive. A cross-seed comparison measures the distance between two
solutions, which is not the effect of an intervention on one of them.

\textbf{The temporal centre of mass survives.} The maximum difference across seed
pairs is $0.115$ time steps, against the $0.93$ produced by a thirtyfold change in
$\gamma$ in the worst class, a factor of eight. The metric sums the map over every
position before reading its time axis, so it is blind to where the activity
sits, which is precisely the property on which the two solutions differ.
Independently trained networks place their activity in different regions of the
input but accumulate it over the eight steps on nearly the same schedule.

\subsection{A negligible perturbation}
\label{sec:perturbation}

The grid compares a quantized model with its own parent, so the operative
threshold is what a perturbation far smaller than quantization already produces.
For this scope, gaussian noise was added to the weight tensors of one checkpoint --- biases and
LIF buffers excluded, since perturbing $\beta$ would confound membrane dynamics
with weight quantization --- at magnitudes expressed as fractions of the measured
8-bit step of those weights ($0.00168$, from a maximum absolute weight of
$0.214$).

This is a graded form of the model randomization test of Adebayo et
al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}: where they randomize weights to verify that a
metric responds at all, this instead perturbs towards zero to find where it stops
responding.

PCC remains above $0.975$ under a perturbation as large as the entire 8-bit step
(Table~\ref{tab:perturbation}). This further supports that the collapse of Table~\ref{tab:seeds} is a
consequence of comparing different initializations, and not a fragility of the
metric.

\textbf{The metrics differ sharply in dynamic range.} Measuring the distance from
perfect agreement, PCC starts $0.025$ away from $1$ and can reach $0.847$: 34
times its own floor. IoU starts $0.296$ away and reaches $0.766$, a factor of
only $2.6$, and is already at $0.704$ under a perturbation 128 times smaller than
the 2-bit step of the same global grid, and at least 26 times smaller than the
2-bit step of any channel. IoU is therefore read as an early indicator that a
change has occurred, and PCC as the graded measure of how large it is. The
temporal centre of mass sits between them, with a factor of $3.9$ between its
numerical floor ($0.029$ steps) and the seed level ($0.115$).

Extremely\_Loud does not respond. Its PCC is $0.9997$ under the largest
perturbation and its worst per-timestep correlation is $0.996$. Combined with its
behaviour under $\gamma$, this class registers no explanation change under any
calibration measure tested.

\subsection{Fine-tuning alone}
\label{sec:anchor}

Every quantized model in the grid is its parent with bits removed \emph{and} then
fine-tuned, which means that part of any divergence measured against that parent belongs to the
fine-tuning. The levels above cannot separate the two contributions: the
numerical floor is a perturbed copy that was never trained, and the
independent-solutions level is two networks grown from different
initializations. To answer the question a model that went through the whole
procedure with nothing removed is needed.

The control arm is that model, \textbf{taken after its last fine-tuning epoch}, not
at the selected checkpoint, which for the control coincides with the parent
(Section~\ref{sec:grid-gates}). Its parameter vector moves by an $L_2$ distance
of $0.395$, $0.736$ and $0.399$ over the three seeds, nine tenths of it in the
last convolution and the linear readout.

rests on. The Pearson correlation of this anchor is $0.987$ against an IoU of

too, where there is no quantization to attribute it to.

the twelfth, Extremely\_Loud on seed 1234, moves by $0.001$. A shift toward
earlier steps in a quantized model is therefore expected from the training alone,
and only its excess over this level, or a shift in the opposite direction, can be
charged to the removed bits.

Table~\ref{tab:calibration} collects the four calibrations and
Table~\ref{tab:levels-class} gives the floor and the fine-tuning level class by
class. The \emph{numerical floor} marks measurement noise: a divergence smaller
than the one a negligible weight perturbation already produces cannot be read.
\emph{Fine-tuning alone} is what the procedure costs when nothing is quantized.
Neither level contains the other. Fine-tuning moves the centre of mass more than
the floor in every class, the correlation more in three, and the overlap less on
Blip and Violin\_Mode but more on Extremely\_Loud and Scattered\_Light. A grid
result is therefore attributed to the removed bits only where it exceeds both
levels of its own class, and the worst-class values of

effect: a PCC of $0.90$ would mean that quantization does more than a thirtyfold
change of the explainer itself. The \emph{independent-solutions} level is a
pessimistic upper bound and a result in its own right.

The class sensitivity ordering --- Violin\_Mode $>$ Scattered\_Light $>$ Blip $>$
Extremely\_Loud --- recurs identically under the two interventions that act on a
fixed network: changing the kernel and perturbing the weights. Nonetheless, it does not
survive a change of initialization, where Scattered\_Light is the most disturbed
class and Violin\_Mode only the third. Within a given
network, explanation sensitivity is a property of the class morphology, so every
grid result should be analysed and reported per class.

\subsection{Clipping the potential costs nothing}

Clipping is an operation that sets a maximum and minimum threshold
in order to truncate extreme values or outliers.
Before any quantization, the potential of the four hidden layers was
\emph{clipped} at every time step to a candidate range, on the full-precision
network, and the effect on accuracy and dynamics was then measured. Clipping isolates
the choice of range from the choice of bit-width: if a range already changes the
network in full precision, no grid built on it could be faithful.

Three observations follow from Table~\ref{tab:clipping}.

\textbf{Negative potentials carry no information the network uses.} Clipping to
$[0, 2]$ removes every negative value --- specifically in layer 3 a range of 47 thresholds ---
and costs one validation sample out of $1\,435$, so a quarter of the spread between
seeds. The per-layer dead fractions move by at most $0.3$ percentage points
(16.1 / 12.5 / 11.3 / 22.5\,\% against 16.1 / 12.6 / 11.3 / 22.8\,\%), and the
largest shift in the temporal centre of mass of any layer's spike counts is
$0.004$ steps, meaning that is well below the $0.029$-step floor measured for the SAM centre of
mass in Section~\ref{sec:calibration} (the two quantities are related but not
identical). Below rest, how far below no longer matters: a
neuron at $-20$ and a neuron at $0$ both need more than one threshold of input
to fire.

\textbf{The upper bound can be tight.} This comes from the
observation that potentials above twice the threshold are
rare and can be clipped at no measurable cost. Since the clipping acts after the
spike decision, it only alters the carried state of neurons that have just fired
well above threshold; the spike they emitted at that step is unaffected.

\textbf{The clipping is not inert.} The sanity range $[-0.5, +0.5]$, which
keeps the potential permanently below threshold after each step, drops accuracy
to $0.848$ and raises the layer-3 firing rate from $0.153$ to $0.227$. This
guarantees that the hook acts on the state the network actually carries, and the null results
above are genuine.

The unsigned range $[0, 2]$ is adopted: it is the tightest range tested that
stays within tolerance, it centres the threshold in the grid, and again it spends no
levels on negative values. Against the symmetric range $[-2, 2]$, which performs
identically, that saves one bit of resolution at 8 and 4 bits, and a factor of
three at 2 bits, where the symmetric grid would leave no level between
rest and threshold values.

\subsection{Gates and control}
\label{sec:grid-gates}

Attaching and removing the quantizer restores the original
weights exactly, and a quantized checkpoint reloads with zero drift. With no
fine-tuning, the joint 8-bit model of seed 1234 reaches $0.98328$ validation
accuracy, which is identical to its parent; indeed, the 8-bit weight arm loses three samples out
of $1\,435$, so inside the four-sample tolerance.

The control arm, which runs the full fine-tuning procedure with no quantizer,
returns a spatial correlation of exactly $1$, an IoU of exactly $1$ and a
centre-of-mass shift of exactly $0$ with its parent, in every class and every
seed. Also, fine-tuning at one tenth of the reference learning rate never improved on the parent's
validation accuracy, so the selected control checkpoint is the parent itself:
the pipeline --- reload, fine-tune, select, re-evaluate --- introduces nothing
with no quantizer attached. The model at the end of
fine-tuning does move, by the amount measured in Section~\ref{sec:anchor}.

The guard of Section~\ref{sec:reading} holds as well: the 8-bit weight arm keeps
a spatial correlation of at least $0.982$ with its parent in every class at every
seed.

\paragraph{At 8 bits the explanation is preserved; at 4 bits it moves slightly
beyond fine-tuning alone.} Every reading below is made class by class against the

Tables~\ref{tab:grid-iou} and~\ref{tab:grid-perclass}.

At 8 bits the weight-only and membrane-only arms stay within the floor of every
class on all three metrics, with one exception: the PCC of Extremely\_Loud,
$0.9993$ and $0.9991$ against a floor of $0.9997$ that leaves almost no room
below it. The joint arm passes the floor in most classes, on PCC in three, on IoU

the experiment can attribute to the removed bits.

weight arm and in all four for the membrane and joint arms, and below the floor
in two, two and four classes respectively. The margins are small. No 4-bit arm
falls more than $0.05$ below the fine-tuning level of a class, less than the
$0.08$ that separates the fine-tuning controls of different seeds ($0.711$ to
$0.792$), and all of it remains far above the independent-solutions level of
$0.234$.

The temporal centre of mass moves in both single-target arms at 4 bits, by
$0.014$ to $0.060$ steps across classes, above the floor and the fine-tuning
level of every class and below the $0.115$ that separates independent solutions.
The joint arm does not: its shifts, $0.009$ to $0.027$, stay within the
fine-tuning level of every class. The direction separates the arms. Fine-tuning
alone moves the SAM energy earlier ($-0.033$ on average); weight quantization
moves it further the same way ($-0.048$), membrane quantization reverses it
($+0.043$), and in the joint arm the two partly cancel ($+0.018$). Part of the
weight-arm shift therefore belongs to the training, while the membrane arm moves
the explanation by about $0.08$ steps relative to the control.

\paragraph{At 2 bits the weight and joint arms explain a different classifier.}
Their IoU falls to $0.34$ and $0.27$ and their centre of mass shifts by more
than the independent-solutions level. Since those models no longer classify, the
divergence of their explanations is a consequence of the collapse, and not a
measurement of explanation fidelity.

\paragraph{The membrane arm at 2 bits is exactly the result the grid was built to find.}
While accuracy is at parity, the IoU with the parent's explanation is $0.438$,
between $34$ and $57\,\%$ below the floor of each class and far below anything
fine-tuning produces. All twelve class-and-seed cells are below the floor of
their class, the highest at $0.642$, and two are below chance (the lowest
$0.102$): for those, the 20\,\% of the image that SAM highlights in the quantized
model overlaps the parent's no more than two random selections would do.

\textbf{The comparison that carries the thesis is between two models with the
same accuracy.} The 4-bit weight model and the 2-bit membrane model both reach
$0.983$ ($0.9835$ and $0.9823$). The first highlights nearly the same region as
its parent, with an IoU of $0.739$; the second reaches only $0.438$. Selected on
accuracy, the two are interchangeable; their explanations are not.

\textbf{The spatial correlation alone would have given a different answer.} The
2-bit membrane model scores a PCC of $0.923$, inside the range of $0.86$ to
$0.99$ that Rabbi et al.~\cite{rabbi2026} report for convolutional models
performing well at INT8, and would be read as a surviving
explanation; it is below the floor of every class, by an amount that understates
what changed. Pearson correlation weighs every position by its value and is
dominated by the broad structure that every map of a class shares; the top-$k$
overlap reads only the ranking, and only above the $(1-k)$ quantile. This is the
disagreement between metrics that Bylinskii et al.~\cite{bylinskii2019metrics}
warn of, and it is why both are reported.

\textbf{The effect depends on the seed.} The 2-bit membrane IoU is $0.255$,
$0.624$ and $0.435$ for the three seeds, a spread of $0.37$, of the size of the
largest effect measured within a seed (the $0.443$ SNR gradient of
Scattered\_Light on seed 1234, Table~\ref{tab:stratified}). The class ordering
is also not the one of the calibration: Blip, and not Violin\_Mode, loses the
most ($0.300$), and Extremely\_Loud, which registered no perturbation in the
calibration, now moves ($0.559$).

\textbf{The effect also depends on $k$.} On seed 3456, the one model for which
the overlap was computed at several mask sizes (Figure~\ref{fig:topk-sweep}), the
2-bit membrane keeps $0.54$ of the parent's top 2\,\% on Blip and $0.47$ on
Scattered\_Light, 53 and 47 times chance, against $0.17$ and $0.50$ at the
reported $k = 0.20$. Extremely\_Loud and Violin\_Mode go the other way, from
$0.16$ and $0.10$ at $k = 0.02$ to $0.61$ and $0.46$. For the compact Blip
stroke the positions SAM ranks highest survive quantization, and what changes is
the ranking of the background that completes the 20\,\% set
(Figures~\ref{fig:sam-qat} and~\ref{fig:app-sam-1234}). The saturated
Extremely\_Loud maps peak on a broad region, where a small $k$ chooses among
near-equal values. How much the explanation changes, and which class it hits
hardest, depend on $k$ as well as on the model.

\subsection{The hypothesis}
\label{sec:hypothesis-result}

The hypothesis predicted an ordering: the membrane arm displaces the temporal
centre of mass at a higher bit-width than the one at which the weight arm lowers
the spatial correlation. The grid does not show that ordering. Both effects first
appear at the same bit-width, 4 bits, and in every class on the mean over seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:grid-perclass}): the weight arm lowers the PCC below both the
floor and the fine-tuning level, and the membrane arm moves the centre of mass
beyond both. At 8 bits neither arm crosses the fine-tuning level of any class on
either metric.

Read cell by cell, against the floor of the class and the fine-tuning level of
the same seed, the reading does not change. At 8 bits the two quantities the
hypothesis orders cross both levels in none of the twelve class-and-seed cells.
At 4 bits the membrane arm moves the centre of mass beyond both in nine of
twelve, the exceptions being Scattered\_Light and Violin\_Mode on seed 1234 and
Extremely\_Loud on seed 3456; the weight arm lowers the PCC below both in ten of
twelve, the exceptions being Violin\_Mode on seeds 2345 and 3456, where it stays
above the floor of that class. The two effects remain simultaneous.

At 2 bits, where the arms do separate, the visible effect of membrane
quantization on the explanation is \emph{spatial} --- which pixels are
highlighted --- while its centre-of-mass shift ($0.068$) stays below the
independent-solutions level. \textbf{As formulated in terms of SAM metrics, the
hypothesis is not supported.}

The per-model validation pass measures a second temporal property, the
regularity of firing, and there the 2-bit membrane differs from every other
configuration.

\textbf{A 2-bit membrane makes the firing of layer 1 regular.} The first
evidence is the coefficient of variation of the inter-spike intervals, pooled
over the partition (Table~\ref{tab:cv-isi-grid}), which in the three parents
lies between $0.35$ and $0.39$. With a 2-bit membrane it falls by about a third,
to $0.24$--$0.25$ in the membrane-only arm and $0.21$--$0.26$ in the joint arm. A
4-bit membrane leaves it within $0.01$ of the parent, and ternary (2-bit) weights
alone lower it as well, but by less than half as much.

The pooled value mixes intervals from different neurons and different images,
so the coefficient is also computed inside each image, over the neurons that
emit at least three spikes in the eight steps. In all six models with a 2-bit
membrane it is \emph{exactly zero} for every such neuron in every image: each
one fires at perfectly regular intervals. Exact regularity is not rare at $T = 8$
even in full precision: in the parents $42$ to $53\,\%$ of the same
neuron-image pairs are exactly regular, and the mean coefficient is $0.153$ to
$0.188$. The 2-bit membrane takes that fraction to $100\,\%$. The two sets of
neurons are comparable in size: $20$ to $53\,\%$ of neuron-image pairs reach
three spikes in the quantized models, $18$ to $51\,\%$ in the parents.

The cause is in the grid of Table~\ref{tab:membrane-grid}. At 2 bits the stored
state takes one of four values, and under direct encoding layer 1 receives the
same input current at every step, so the next stored state is a function of the
current one alone. Every trajectory therefore falls into a cycle within a few
steps, and the spike train repeats with it. Deeper layers receive spike trains
that vary from step to step and are not constrained in the same way.

Membrane quantization does act on the temporal dynamics, as the hypothesis
argued, but not before the spatial explanation changes: the regularity appears
at 2 bits, where the overlap of the explanation also collapses, and not at 4
bits, where the pooled coefficient stays within $0.01$ of the parent. The
per-image coefficient was not measured at 4 bits. What a 2-bit membrane changes
is the \emph{regularity} of the spike trains. The temporal centre of mass
measures where in time the SAM energy falls, not how regularly the spikes
arrive, and cannot detect the change.

The firing statistics separate the two arms (Figure~\ref{fig:spike-stats}). In
the membrane-only arm the 2-bit membrane raises the firing rate of every layer,
from $0.258 / 0.186 / 0.185 / 0.126$ to $0.273 / 0.218 / 0.229 / 0.151$, and
moves the dead fraction by at most four points. Its regularizing effect is
confined to layer 1: in layers 2 to 4 the pooled coefficient of variation rises
by $3$, $9$ and $6\,\%$. The 2-bit weights double the dead fraction of layer 4,
from $23.6$ to $43.1\,\%$, and raise the saturated fraction of layer 1 from
$4.7\,\%$ in the parents to $13\,\%$ on average, higher in every seed and
$22\,\%$ in the worst. They lower the coefficient of variation in every layer,
by $21\,\%$ in layer 4, but there only $60\,\%$ of the sampled positions keep
five intervals, against $88\,\%$ in the parents: activity disappearing rather
than becoming regular.

\textbf{The explanation is changed, and not invalidated.} 
In every interpretable cell the
quantized model's own map still separates from random deletion: the median gap
is $0.94$ of the parent's, and only one cell comes close to losing it --- Blip
on seed 3456, from $0.113$ to $0.043$. Explaining the quantized model with its parent's maps works about as well as with
its own: the mean difference between the two gaps is $+0.012$ over the seven
cells, five positive and two negative, never more than $0.049$, although the
top-20\,\% regions of the two maps overlap with an IoU of only $0.438$.

The two measurements are therefore not in contradiction, but different, 
and they answer different questions. 
The deletion metric rewards any map that points at evidence the model
uses, and when the evidence is redundant --- like in a Blip where stroke covers many
positions--- several disjoint sets of pixels
satisfy it. The IoU instead says which of those sets a reader is shown.

\textbf{Which classes SAM can explain at all depends on the seed.} Only seven of
the twelve class-and-seed cells have a baseline signal: Extremely\_Loud in all
three parents, Blip in two, Scattered\_Light and Violin\_Mode in one each. Even
Blip's baseline gap ranges from $0.029$ to $0.171$ across networks of equal
accuracy. Therefore the set of classes on which faithfulness can be assessed is a property
of the individual network, not of the class.

\subsection{Firing-rate distance and the disagreement subset}
\label{sec:disagreement}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{behaviour_vs_explanation.png}
\caption{Left: each point is one quantized model; abscissa, mean top-20\,\% IoU
with its parent; ordinate, Jaccard overlap between the error sets of model and
parent; marker size, bit-width. Dashed horizontal line and band: the same overlap
between full-precision models of different seed (mean $0.49$, range
$0.43$--$0.53$); dashed vertical line: the worst-class numerical floor. Right:
Wasserstein-1 distance between the per-neuron firing-rate distributions of the
2-bit models and their parents, per layer; dash-dot line and band: the same
distance between full-precision seeds, mean and range over the three pairs.}
\label{fig:behaviour}
\end{figure}

The two measurements read the grid on the network's own terms 
(against the seed-to-seed reference of
Section~\ref{sec:hyperparameters}) rather than on the explanation's.

The Wasserstein-1 distance between the per-neuron firing-rate distributions of a
quantized model and its parent is read layer by layer against the distance
between full-precision seeds. At 8 bits every layer stays below $0.015$ on
average. At 4 bits the values reach $0.030$ in layer 3 and $0.023$ in layer 4,
inside the range of the seed pairs in every layer and, in layer 4, at its mean.
The 2-bit membrane stays well below the seed level in layers 1 and 2 ($0.019$
and $0.032$) and exceeds its mean in layers 3 and 4 ($0.045$ and $0.025$),
remaining inside the range of the pairs in layer 3 and above it in layer 4. The
2-bit weight arms reach $0.148$ and $0.161$ in layer 3, about three times the
largest distance between seeds. Down to 4 bits, then, quantization moves the
firing regime no more than a change of initialization does, the comparison
Smith et al.~\cite{smith2026} draw; at 2 bits the membrane does so only in the
deep layers.

The disagreement subset says the same of the decisions. The Jaccard overlap
between the error sets of a quantized model and its parent is $0.78$--$0.80$ at
8 bits in the single-target arms and $0.62$--$0.70$ at 4 bits and at the 2-bit
membrane, against $0.49$ between full-precision models of different seed and
$0.03$ in the collapsed arms. Of the $1\,435$ validation images, three to six on
average are correct in the parent and wrong after quantization at 8 and 4 bits,
six at the 2-bit membrane, against $671$ in the 2-bit weight arms. \textbf{The
2-bit membrane model keeps most of its parent's decisions and explains them with
different pixels}: about six images go from correct to wrong and five the other
way, and its error set overlaps the parent's with a Jaccard index of $0.58$ to
$0.69$ across seeds, so roughly a third of the errors of the two models are not
shared. Figure~\ref{fig:behaviour} places every model on both axes: the 2-bit membrane
models keep the decision overlap of the 4-bit models and lose most of the
explanation overlap.

\textbf{The membrane arm does not save energy; at 2 bits it costs more.} Its
weights stay in full precision, so the cost of each operation never falls, and
the operation count rises: $+1.5\,\%$ at 8 bits, $+4.4\,\%$ at 4 and
$+15.3\,\%$ at 2 against the parent, and the energy with it, at $1.011$,
$1.031$ and $1.108$ times the parent (Table~\ref{tab:energy-grid}). What it does
save is the state memory, 344\,KiB at 2 bits, almost a quarter of the total
footprint.

Part of that rise belongs to the training rather than to the bits. Fine-tuning
alone, measured on the control after its last epoch, raises the operation count
by $5.0$ to $6.2\,\%$, from $1$--$3\,\%$ more spikes in layer 1 to
$10$--$22\,\%$ more in layer 4. Measured against that state, with every run also
taken at its last epoch (\texttt{results/energy\_final.json}), the 8-bit
configurations change the count by $-0.3$ to $+0.5\,\%$, the 4-bit ones by
$+2.5$ to $+6.6\,\%$, the 2-bit membrane by $+11\,\%$ and the 2-bit weights by
$+48$ and $+51\,\%$, the collapsed networks firing more. At 8 bits the removed
bits change the operation count by nothing. The 2-bit membrane costs $1.08$
times the control in energy, a rise that belongs to the quantized state. In the
weight and joint arms the saving comes entirely from the cheaper operations.

\textbf{This grid did not find a trade-off between efficiency and explainability.} 
The configuration that changes the explanation at constant accuracy, the
2-bit membrane, is dominated on every axis by the 4-bit weight model: same
accuracy, IoU $0.739$ against $0.438$, energy $0.017$ against $1.108$ times the
parent, memory $512$ against $1\,176$\,KiB. 

The configuration to deploy is the \textbf{joint 4-bit model}: accuracy at parity
($0.9835$), an explanation that moves slightly beyond what continued training
alone produces (IoU $0.714$ against $0.750$, below the fine-tuning level of every
class by at most $0.05$ and of every seed by $0.02$ to $0.06$, against $0.438$
for the 2-bit membrane), estimated energy about $1/60$ of the parent under the
costing of Table~\ref{tab:energy-grid}, and one eighth of its memory
($190$\,KiB).

A few assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:energy-method} determine the size of the
saving. The first convolution receives the analog image, and
Table~\ref{tab:energy-grid} costs it at the weight bit-width, which assumes the
input is quantized to the same width. At 4 bits that layer is $24\,\%$ of the
joint model's energy. Instead, costed in floating point, it lowers the joint 4-bit
saving factor from $59$ to $2.8$, $4.2$ and $3.3$ on the three seeds, whose
firing regimes differ. The accumulates are costed at the weight width too. With
16-bit integer additions the joint 4-bit factor becomes $20$ to $23$ across
seeds, with 32-bit additions $10$ to $12$, and with the floating-point first
layer and 32-bit additions together $2.4$ to $3.2$. Under 16-bit additions the
2-bit models, which fire more, are no longer the cheapest: the joint 2-bit factor
falls to $16$, below the $21$ of the joint 4-bit model.

The operation counts and the memory column depend on neither assumption, and
neither does the conclusion that the membrane arm is dominated, since its
weights and accumulates stay in floating point under every costing. The factor
by which the deployable model is cheaper does depend on them, by more than an
order of magnitude.

\section{Test-set evaluation}
\label{sec:test}

The test partition was opened once, after every decision in this thesis had been
taken on validation, and all thirty-three models of the grid were evaluated on it
in a single pass. The test accuracy was computed twice by independent routes: from the
aggregated evaluation loop and from the per-sample predictions, and the two agree
to within $10^{-6}$ for every model.

\textbf{Neither quantized configuration costs measurable accuracy on held-out

hyperparameters, the stopping epoch and the selected checkpoint of every model
