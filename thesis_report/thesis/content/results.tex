The ordering in Table~\ref{tab:resolution-accuracy} is not monotonic, as 64\,px
outperforms 112\,px, and a genuine resolution effect would produce monotonic
degradation. $0.9854$ corresponds to $21.0$ errors out of
$1\,435$ validation samples and $0.9814$ to $26.7$, a gap of $5.7$ samples,
against a 95\,\% Wilson interval on a single measurement of about $0.7$
percentage points. The four classes survive a twelvefold reduction in pixel
count, which further confirm their being distinguished by coarse morphology.

Per-class recall is directional but small. Violin\_Mode degrades most from
224\,px to 64\,px, by $1.00$ percentage points, which is the direction predicted
from morphology since narrow spectral lines are the structure most vulnerable to
downsampling --- but the magnitude is $3.7$ samples out of $365$, inside the
confidence interval of a single measurement. Extremely\_Loud degrades more at
112\,px than at 64\,px, a second non-monotonicity. The single most 
relevant observation is that \textbf{no class collapses at
reduced resolution.}

\subsection{Network health does discriminate}

Since accuracy is uninformative, the resolution was decided on the internal state
of the network. For each hidden layer, the mean firing rate of every individual
neuron is accumulated across the validation partition: a rate below $0.01$
identifies a \emph{dead} neuron, above $0.80$ a \emph{saturated} one.

The two ends are pathological for different reasons. A neuron firing on fewer
than one input in a hundred is wasted capacity and, being silent, cannot appear
in any explanation; its output is near-deterministic, carrying $0.081$ of the
one bit a binary unit can hold. Saturation is not an information problem itself --- a
Bernoulli variable at $0.80$ retains $0.722$ bits, as much as one at $0.20$ ---
but an energy one: the energy of a spiking layer scales with the number of
spikes it emits~\cite{suetake2023penalty}, so a unit near its ceiling
is essentially spending the
sparsity the architecture was chosen for.

Neither cutoff level (dead and saturated) is derived.
Thresholds of this kind are conventions elsewhere
too: the pruning literature trims neurons whose fraction of zero activations
exceeds one standard deviation of the layer mean~\cite{hu2016trimming}, and the
dormant-neuron analysis treats the cutoff as a tuned hyperparameter, reporting
values between $0$ and $0.1$~\cite{sokar2023dormant}. Sweeping both cutoffs on
the adopted configuration shows no plateau (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}). A factor of
two either side of $0.01$ moves the dead fraction by $11.2$, $9.0$, $6.7$ and
$11.2$ percentage points in the four layers, so the distribution of rates holds
no gap separating dead units from live ones. The dead fraction is the value of a
monotone function read at a conventional point, 
and it carries meaning only as a comparison at fixed
cutoff, which is how Table~\ref{tab:dead} uses it.

Layer 4 carries the resolution effect. The dead fraction falls monotonically 
from $36.3$\,\% to $19.5$\,\%, while
the median per-neuron firing rate rises from $0.0399$ to $0.1499$. These were the
two competing explanations for the higher mean activity observed at lower
resolution --- more neurons participating, or the same neurons firing harder ---
and both occur. Layer 2 also falls monotonically, by $5.4$ percentage points
against layer 4's $16.8$; layers 1 and 3 are not monotonic at all. Saturation instead 
does not increase to compensate: neurons above $0.80$ remain below $2.5$\,\% in
layer 4 at every resolution.

\textbf{The interpretation of this effect is attributed to spatial redundancy.} 
Layer 4
contains $25\,088$ neurons at 224\,px and $6\,272$ at 112\,px, so in absolute
terms the number of \emph{live} neurons falls from roughly $15\,970$ to $4\,789$.
Reducing the resolution does not increase usable capacity: it reduces capacity
while preferentially discarding units that were contributing nothing. At 224\,px
the deepest layer views a $14 \times 14$ map derived from a largely empty
spectrogram, and a third of its units never encounter signal.

\subsection{Decision: 112 pixels}

Most of the benefit comes from the first halving: 224 to 112\,px recovers $12.7$
percentage points of dead fraction, the further move to 64\,px only recovers $4.1$ more.
Membrane state at 8 bits falls from $368$ to $92$\,KiB --- the state of all four
hidden layers held at once, which is the part of the footprint that must sit in
fast memory and be rewritten at every one of the $T$ steps, the weights being
read-only.

The decisive argument against 64\,px is the spatial resolution of the
explanations themselves. SAM maps are computed on layer activations, whose side
lengths are 56, 28, 14 and 7 at 112\,px but 32, 16, 8 and 4 at 64\,px. A
$4 \times 4$ map contains sixteen values, on which centre-of-mass, top-$K$
intersection-over-union and structural similarity are all meaningless. Given that the
contribution of this thesis is the fidelity of explanations, 
a configuration in which the measured object is degenerate in one
layer cannot be accepted, whatever it saves in compute.

\section{Hyperparameters and the full-precision reference}
\label{sec:hyperparameters}

The constrained search selected \textbf{$\beta = 0.75$, $T = 8$, learning rate
$1.047 \times 10^{-4}$} at a best validation accuracy of $0.9847$ within the
15-epoch budget.

\textbf{The constraint on $\beta$ is justified empirically.} 
An unconstrained search over $\beta \in (0,1)$ selects $0.4148$, whose
nearest admissible neighbours are $0.25$ and $0.5$. The constrained search
selects $0.75$. Rounding the unconstrained optimum after the fact --- the
alternative to constraining the space --- would therefore have produced a
configuration that is both inferior and never validated.

\textbf{$T = 8$ is an optimum at the imposed ceiling}:
the search was forbidden from exploring $T > 8$. The mitigating evidence is that
an unconstrained search with range up to 15 selected $T = 7$, so the preference
for long integration windows is mild.

\subsection{The operating point improves the measurement instrument}

Holding resolution and partition fixed and moving from the unconstrained
configuration ($\beta = 0.4148$, $T = 7$) to the adopted one ($\beta = 0.75$,
$T = 8$), the layer-4 dead fraction is \emph{unchanged} at $23.6$\,\%. The decay
constant does not affect the dead fraction, but resolution does. This 
represents another point in favor to the
reading of Section~\ref{sec:resolution}: the deep-layer problem is spatial
redundancy, and not a dynamical pathology curable by a better decay constant.

What the change affects is saturation, by an order of magnitude and most
where it matters. Neurons above $0.80$ fall from $9.3 / 3.5 / 2.7 / 1.8$\,\%
across the four layers to $4.7 / 0.22 / 0.06 / 0.14$\,\%. The layer-2 figure 
(where the SAM map is computed) is
the consequential one. A saturated neuron fires at nearly
every step. It is then almost always firing at $t$, and always arrives there
with a long spike history, so it accumulates the maximum
possible contribution score on every image and therefore contributes a constant,
non-discriminative floor to every SAM map. \textbf{Selecting on validation
accuracy alone happened to reduce that contamination by a factor of sixteen at
the reference layer.}

The reference is statistically indistinguishable from the 224\,px configuration
($0.9854$): three samples out of $1\,435$ separate them, against a four-sample

constraining $\beta$ and $T$ to hardware-realizable values costs no measurable
accuracy.}

\textbf{Accuracy is reproducible, while the internal dynamics are not.} At fixed accuracy
the mean firing rate of layer 1 varies by a factor of $2.6$ across seeds, and the
coefficient of variation falls monotonically with depth: $46.9$\,\% at layer 1,
$28.6$\,\% at layer 2, $15.3$\,\% at layer 3, $9.5$\,\% at layer 4. Furthermore, this regime is
established within the first epoch and persists, which indicates that the initialization
selects the firing regime and training does not subsequently equalize it. In the
units used for the grid comparison of Section~\ref{sec:disagreement}, the
Wasserstein-1 distance between the per-neuron firing-rate distributions of two
full-precision models differing only in seed is $0.160$ at layer 1, and then falls with
depth to $0.022$ at layer 4, averaged over the three pairs. Section~\ref{sec:disagreement}
reads every quantized model against those figures. This is the observation that
motivates Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.

$0.994 / 0.986 / 0.984 / 0.970$, consistent with Table~\ref{tab:fp32}; the only
non-negligible errors are on Violin\_Mode, the class that in the tabular domain
partially overlaps both Blip and Scattered\_Light (Figure~\ref{fig:confusion}).

The dead fraction is $16.1 / 12.6 / 11.3 / 22.8$\,\% across the four layers
(Figure~\ref{fig:firing}). Saturation is confined to the first layer and to

different script from a separate forward pass, so both are verified at no cost.

A note worth mentioning is that the layer-1 distribution is \textbf{bimodal}, with a
secondary population near a firing rate of $0.65$--$0.75$ that has no counterpart
in the deeper layers. A high-activity subpopulation therefore exists in the first
layer only, which may be related to its being the layer with the largest
coefficient of variation across seeds.

The coefficient of variation of inter-spike intervals, pooled over the partition,

This is an evidence about internal dynamics.

threshold used for the estimate is close to the ceiling, which is why the
accumulation runs over the whole partition and not on a single image. Between

\section{Fixing the explainer}

\subsection{Choice of the decay rate}
\label{sec:gamma}

SAM has one free parameter, $\gamma$, which represents 
\textbf{the decay of a temporal kernel, so it must be evaluated on
the temporal axis.} Comparing maps averaged over time removes precisely the axis
on which $\gamma$ acts, and a high spatial correlation is then close to
guaranteed regardless of $\gamma$. Three quantities are therefore measured: the
spatial Pearson correlation between time-aggregated maps, retained for continuity
with the literature; the \emph{temporal centre of mass} of the SAM energy; and
the \emph{per-timestep correlation} against a reference $\gamma$.

These metrics were checked analytically:
on a fully firing spike train with $T = 8$ the centre of mass moves from $4.86$
at $\gamma = 0.1$ to $4.02$ at $\gamma = 3.0$, so the metric does respond. A
second check comes free from the definition. At $t = 1$ the score is
$e^{-\gamma} S_0$, so changing $\gamma$ multiplies the map by a constant, and
Pearson correlation is affine-invariant. The measured per-timestep correlation at
$t = 1$ is exactly $1.000$ for every $\gamma$.

Two main conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:gamma}. 
\textbf{The temporal shift is kernel arithmetic, and not explanation structure.} The
measured shift of $0.83$--$0.93$ steps is what a \emph{fully firing} spike train
produces, $0.84$. It is also what a random train produces at any density: $0.84$
at a firing rate of $0.05$, $0.83$ at $0.19$, the rate measured in layer 2, and
$0.84$ at $0.70$. The independence is exact because with
Bernoulli trains at rate $p$ the expected SAM energy at step $t$ is
$p^{2}$ times a function of $\gamma$ alone, and the $p^{2}$ cancels in the ratio
that defines the centre of mass. Changing $\gamma$ therefore moves the centre of
mass by an amount that carries no information about which neurons fired or when.
For this reason the centre of mass against $\gamma$ is \emph{not} a criterion for
selecting $\gamma$ --- though it remains valid as a divergence metric, where
models are compared at fixed $\gamma$ and the trivial component cancels.

\textbf{Sensitivity is strongly class-dependent.} In terms of $1 - \mathrm{PCC}$,
Violin\_Mode is 57 times more sensitive than Extremely\_Loud. The ordering
Violin\_Mode $>$ Scattered\_Light $>$ Blip $>$ Extremely\_Loud recurs under the
weight perturbation of Section~\ref{sec:perturbation}.

Within $\gamma \in [0.3, 0.9]$ all per-timestep correlations remain above $0.98$
even in the worst class, and only $\gamma = 3.0$ diverges appreciably.
\textbf{$\gamma = 0.5$ is adopted}, the value of the original
formulation~\cite{kim2021sam}, which is also supported by evidence on the axis $\gamma$
 governs (Figure~\ref{fig:gamma}).

\subsection{Faithfulness}
\label{sec:faithfulness}

Robustness to $\gamma$ is necessary but not sufficient, since a stable explanation could
still be stably wrong. The deletion metric~\cite{petsiuk2018rise} removes pixels in
decreasing order of SAM score and records the confidence assigned to the class
predicted on the unmodified image, and comparing against the removal of pixel in random order.

Deleted pixels are set to
zero \emph{in normalized space}, corresponding to the dataset mean which
functions as a neutral substrate. 
The same map
is used for both orderings, and the ordering is computed once per image and
sliced progressively, so the masked sets are nested: what is removed at 50\,\%
stays removed at 55\,\%.

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Deletion metric by class on the reference checkpoint, 15 validation

\label{tab:deletion}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

\caption{Deletion curves, SAM order (blue) against random order (grey), 15 validation images

The metric separates SAM from the baseline on two classes and not on the other
two, and the split is systematic (Table~\ref{tab:deletion},
Figure~\ref{fig:deletion}). Blip and Extremely\_Loud show reductions of $35$ and
$32$\,\% in area. On Scattered\_Light and Violin\_Mode deleting the
pixels SAM ranks highest destroys confidence \emph{more slowly} than deleting
pixels at random, by $10.1$ and $5.4$\,\% of area, which is roughly a third and a
sixth of the $35$ and $32$\,\% by which it runs faster on the other two classes.

The pattern follows again the spatial geometry of the classes. 
Blip is a compact vertical line and Extremely\_Loud a dominant saturated
structure, so removing the high-scoring region removes the defining evidence.
On the other hand
Scattered\_Light is an extended horizontal band, and Violin\_Mode a set of thin
distributed lines: the interpretation is that random deletion breaks 
the pattern across the whole image,
whereas SAM-guided deletion concentrates removal in one region and leaves the
rest --- which still carries sufficient evidence --- intact.

The random baseline of Extremely\_Loud, $0.8936$, is the highest of the four and
is independent confirmation of that class's behaviour that its evidence is
distributed across the whole image.

\section{Calibrating the divergence metrics}
\label{sec:calibration}

Since the metrics used to compare two explanations return numbers between zero and one
whatever is fed to them. Three questions must be answered before any quantization
result can be interpreted: what they report when nothing has changed, when
something negligible has changed, and when everything has changed.

All measurements use fifteen validation images per class at layer 2 with
$\gamma = 0.5$, on the three checkpoints of Table~\ref{tab:fp32}.

\subsection{Verification of the instrument}

The verification comes from the control arm, whose selected checkpoint is
bit-identical to its parent (Section~\ref{sec:grid-gates}): compared against that
parent the metrics return $\mathrm{PCC} = 1$, $\mathrm{IoU} = 1$ and a
centre-of-mass difference of $0$, in every class and every seed. This means that two models that
are the same model therefore produce the same explanation under the full
comparison pipeline, image sampling included, which is what the measurement
assumes. The network is evaluated with gradients disabled and in inference mode,
and the image sampler is seeded, so the cross-model differences below cannot be
run-to-run noise.

\subsection{Two independent solutions}

\textbf{The spatial metrics collapse.} Two models agreeing to within one
validation sample in accuracy produce maps correlating at $0.153$ on
Scattered\_Light, with a top-20\,\% overlap of $0.234$ against a chance level of
$0.111$. For comparison, a thirtyfold change in $\gamma$ reduces the correlation
only to $0.960$: so initialization variance is 21 times larger than that effect.

The collapse is also not uniform across pairs. On Blip the three seed pairs
correlate at $0.34$, $0.72$ and $0.78$, so which two networks are compared
matters more than which class is compared.

These results do not mean that the mechanism is a defect of the metric. 
Networks trained from different
initializations learn functionally equivalent but \textbf{permuted}
representations, so a pixel-wise comparison between them essentially compares arbitrary
assignments of channels to positions. The same
permutation explains why the class ordering of Section~\ref{sec:gamma} does not
survive here: across seeds the most disturbed class is Scattered\_Light, ahead of
Blip and then Violin\_Mode, whereas within a fixed network Violin\_Mode is always
the most sensitive. A cross-seed comparison measures the relabelling, not the
explanation.

\textbf{The temporal centre of mass survives.} The maximum difference across seed
pairs is $0.115$ time steps, against again the $0.93$ produced by a thirtyfold change
in $\gamma$ in the worst class --- a factor of eight. The interpretation here is that
the metric being an aggregate over the
whole map, it is insensitive to which neuron occupies which position, which is
precisely what defeats the pixel-wise metrics.

\subsection{A negligible perturbation}
\label{sec:perturbation}

The grid compares a quantized model with its own parent, so the operative
threshold is what a perturbation far smaller than quantization already produces.
For this scope, gaussian noise was added to the weight tensors of one checkpoint --- biases and
LIF buffers excluded, since perturbing $\beta$ would confound membrane dynamics
with weight quantization --- at magnitudes expressed as fractions of the measured
8-bit step of those weights ($0.00168$, from a maximum absolute weight of
$0.214$).

This is a graded form of the model randomization test of Adebayo et
al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}: where they randomize weights to verify that a
metric responds at all, this instead perturbs towards zero to find where it stops
responding.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering

behaviour under $\gamma$, this class ha been found to not register an explanation change by any
measure tested.

\subsection{Fine-tuning alone}
\label{sec:anchor}

Every quantized model in the grid is its parent with bits removed \emph{and} then
fine-tuned, which means that part of any divergence measured against that parent belongs to the
fine-tuning. The levels above cannot separate the two contributions: the
numerical floor is a perturbed copy that was never trained, and the
independent-solutions level is two networks grown from different
initializations. To answer the question a model that went through the whole
procedure with nothing removed is needed.

The control arm is that model, \textbf{taken after its last fine-tuning epoch}, not
at the selected checkpoint, which for the control coincides with the parent
(Section~\ref{sec:grid-gates}). Its parameter vector moves by an $L_2$ distance
of $0.395$, $0.736$ and $0.399$ over the three seeds, nine tenths of it in the
last convolution and the linear readout.

\subsection{Clipping the potential costs nothing}

Clipping is an operation that sets a maximum and minimum threshold
in order to truncate extreme values or outliers.
Before any quantization, the potential of the four hidden layers was
\emph{clipped} at every time step to a candidate range, on the full-precision
network, and the effect on accuracy and dynamics was then measured. Clipping isolates
the choice of range from the choice of bit-width: if a range already changes the
network in full precision, no grid built on it could be faithful.

\label{tab:clipping}

Three observations follow from Table~\ref{tab:clipping}.

\textbf{Negative potentials carry no information the network uses.} Clipping to
$[0, 2]$ removes every negative value --- specifically in layer 3 a range of 47 thresholds ---
and costs one validation sample out of $1\,435$, so a quarter of the spread between
seeds. The per-layer dead fractions move by at most $0.3$ percentage points
(16.1 / 12.5 / 11.3 / 22.5\,\% against 16.1 / 12.6 / 11.3 / 22.8\,\%), and the
largest shift in the temporal centre of mass of any layer's spike counts is
$0.004$ steps, meaning that is well below the $0.029$-step floor measured for the SAM centre of
mass in Section~\ref{sec:calibration} (the two quantities are related but not
identical). Below rest, how far below no longer matters: a
neuron at $-20$ and a neuron at $0$ both need more than one threshold of input
to fire.

\textbf{The upper bound can be tight.} This comes from the
observation that potentials above twice the threshold are
rare and can be clipped at no measurable cost. Since the clipping acts after the
spike decision, it only alters the carried state of neurons that have just fired
well above threshold; the spike they emitted at that step is unaffected.

\textbf{The clipping is not inert.} The sanity range $[-0.5, +0.5]$, which
keeps the potential permanently below threshold after each step, drops accuracy
to $0.848$ and raises the layer-3 firing rate from $0.153$ to $0.227$. This
guarantees that the hook acts on the state the network actually carries, and the null results
above are genuine.

The unsigned range $[0, 2]$ is adopted: it is the tightest range tested that
stays within tolerance, it centres the threshold in the grid, and again it spends no
levels on negative values. Against the symmetric range $[-2, 2]$, which performs
identically, that saves one bit of resolution at 8 and 4 bits, and a factor of
three at 2 bits, where the symmetric grid would leave no level between
rest and threshold values.

$2.5$ times less. The gap comes
from the price of each operation, not from their number.

The saving depends primarily on the
treatment of the first layer, as it is the only layer that multiplie.
Recomputing its constant current at every step

models indistinguishable in accuracy therefore differ by more than half in
estimated energy, so every energy comparison in the grid is made at matched
seed.

244 biases, kept in full precision, are negligible), and the membrane state of
the $94\,080$ hidden neurons another $367.5$\,KiB. At 8, 4 and 2
bits these values drop to $288$, $144$ and $72$\,KiB for the weights and $92$, $46$ and

\subsection{Gates and control}
\label{sec:grid-gates}

Attaching and removing the quantizer restores the original
weights exactly, and a quantized checkpoint reloads with zero drift. With no
fine-tuning, the joint 8-bit model of seed 1234 reaches $0.98328$ validation
accuracy, which is identical to its parent; indeed, the 8-bit weight arm loses three samples out
of $1\,435$, so inside the four-sample tolerance.

The control arm, which runs the full fine-tuning procedure with no quantizer,
returns a spatial correlation of exactly $1$, an IoU of exactly $1$ and a
centre-of-mass shift of exactly $0$ with its parent, in every class and every
seed. Also, fine-tuning at one tenth of the reference learning rate never improved on the parent's
validation accuracy, so the selected control checkpoint is the parent itself:
the pipeline --- reload, fine-tune, select, re-evaluate --- introduces nothing
with no quantizer attached. The model at the end of
fine-tuning does move, by the amount measured in Section~\ref{sec:anchor}.

The guard of Section~\ref{sec:reading} holds as well: the 8-bit weight arm keeps
a spatial correlation of at least $0.982$ with its parent in every class at every
seed.

the two thresholds separate the two bit-widths. At 8

by any amount the experiment can attribute. At 4 bits all three arms fall between
the two levels, from $0.714$ to $0.740$: so above the numerical floor of $0.704$, and

produces on its own, and it is not separable from it. 

All of this remains far
above the independent-solutions level of $0.234$. The temporal centre of mass is
the only metric that moves at 4 bits, by $0.044$ to $0.049$ steps in the two
single-target arms, meaning that is above its floor of $0.029$ and below the $0.115$ that
separates independent solutions. The direction is informative there: quantizing the weights shifts the SAM

membrane toward later ones, and in the joint arm the two shifts partly cancel
($+0.018$)-. At 8 bits instead, every centre-of-mass shift is under
the floor, so no direction can be read.

\paragraph{At 2 bits the weight and joint arms explain a different classifier.}
Their IoU falls to $0.34$ and $0.27$ and their centre of mass shifts by more
than the independent-solutions level. Since those models no longer classify, the
divergence of their explanations is a consequence of the collapse, and not a pure
indicator of a measurement of explanation fidelity .

\paragraph{The membrane arm at 2 bits is exactly the result the grid was built to find.}
While accuracy is at parity, the IoU with the parent's explanation is $0.438$, meaning a
38\,\% below the numerical floor, and far below anything fine-tuning produces.
All twelve class-and-seed cells are below the floor, and two are below chance
(the lowest $0.102$): for those, the 20\,\% of the image that SAM highlights in
the quantized model overlaps the parent's no more than two random selections
would do.

\textbf{The comparison that carries the thesis is between two models with the
same accuracy.} The 4-bit weight model and the 2-bit membrane model both reach
$0.983$ ($0.9835$ and $0.9823$), but while first explains its decisions with
essentially the same pixels as its parent (IoU $0.739$), the second shares less
than half of them (IoU $0.438$). Selected on accuracy, the two are
interchangeable; their explanations however are not.

\textbf{The spatial correlation alone would have given a different and wrong answer.} The
2-bit membrane model scores a PCC of $0.923$, and, if read against the values commonly
presented as preserved explanations, that figure would be reported as a surviving
explanation; it is below the numerical floor, but by an amount that understates
what changed. Pearson correlation is indeed dominated by the broad, low-intensity
structure that every map of a class shares, while the top-$k$ overlap measures
the region a reader is actually shown. This is the disagreement between metrics
that Bylinskii et al.~\cite{bylinskii2019metrics} warn of, and it is why both are
reported.

\textbf{The effect depends on the seed.} The 2-bit membrane IoU is $0.255$,
$0.624$ and $0.435$ for the three seeds, which characterize a spread larger than any effect
measured within a seed below. The class ordering is also not the one of the
calibration: Blip, and not Violin\_Mode, loses the most ($0.300$), and
Extremely\_Loud, which registered no perturbation in the calibration, now moves
($0.559$).

\subsection{The hypothesis}
\label{sec:hypothesis-result}

The hypothesis predicted an ordering: the membrane arm displaces the temporal
centre of mass at a higher bit-width than the one at which the weight arm lowers
the spatial correlation. But the grid does not show that ordering. Both effects
first appear at the same bit-width, 4 bits, where the weight arm lowers the PCC
below the per-class floor on every class and both
single-target arms shift the centre of mass by the same amount. Conversely, at 2 bits, where
the arms do separate, the visible effect of membrane quantization on the
explanation is \emph{spatial} --- so which pixels are highlighted --- while its
centre-of-mass shift ($0.068$) stays below the independent-solutions level.
\textbf{As formulated in terms of SAM metrics, the hypothesis is not supported.}

Its mechanism, however, is visible elsewhere: the per-model validation pass
measures the regularity of firing, and there the membrane arm at 2 bits differs
from other configurations.

\label{tab:cv-isi-grid}

\textbf{A 2-bit membrane makes the firing of layer 1 periodic.} The first
evidence is the coefficient of variation of the inter-spike intervals, pooled
over the partition (Table~\ref{tab:cv-isi-grid}), that in the three parents it lies
between $0.35$ and $0.39$. With a 2-bit membrane it falls by about a third, to
$0.24$--$0.25$ in the membrane-only arm and $0.21$--$0.26$ in the joint arm. A
4-bit membrane leaves it within $0.01$ of the parent, and ternary weights (2-bit) alone lower
it as well, but by less than half as much.

The pooled value mixes intervals from different neurons and different images, for this
reason a measurement inside each image is needed. This is performed over 
the neurons that emit at least three spikes in
the eight steps, and here the resulting coefficient is \emph{exactly zero}:
meaning that in all six models with a
2-bit membrane, every such neuron fires at perfectly regular intervals in every
image. The parents give $0.153$ to $0.188$ under the same protocol. The two sets
of neurons are comparable in size: $20$ to $53$\,\% of neuron-image pairs reach
three spikes in the quantized models, $18$ to $51$\,\% in the parents.

The cause is in the grid of Table~\ref{tab:membrane-grid}: since at 2 bits a single
level lies between rest and threshold, between two spikes the state of a
neuron can take only one intermediate value. Under direct encoding layer 1
receives the same input current at every step, the number of steps from reset to
threshold is therefore fixed, and the firing becomes periodic.

Membrane quantization does act first on the temporal dynamics, as the hypothesis
argued; what it changes is the \emph{regularity} of the spike trains. The
temporal centre of mass measures where in time the SAM energy falls, not how
regularly the spikes arrive, and cannot detect the change.

The firing statistics separate the two arms. In the membrane-only arm the 2-bit
membrane raises the firing rate of every layer, from
$0.258 / 0.186 / 0.185 / 0.126$ to $0.273 / 0.218 / 0.229 / 0.151$, with little
change in the dead fraction. The 2-bit weights double the dead 
fraction of layer 4, from $23.6$ to $43.1\,\%$, and
raise the saturated fraction of layer 1 from $4.7\,\%$ in the parents to $13\,\%$
on average, higher in every seed and $22\,\%$ in the worst.

\textbf{The explanation is changed, and not invalidated.} 
In every interpretable cell the
quantized model's own map still separates from random deletion: the median gap
is $0.94$ of the parent's, and only one cell comes close to losing it --- Blip

explaining the quantized model with its

never more than $0.049$, although the two maps share only 44\,\% of their

The two measurements are therefore not in contradiction, but different, 
and they answer different questions. 
The deletion metric rewards any map that points at evidence the model
uses, and when the evidence is redundant --- like in a Blip where stroke covers many
positions--- several disjoint sets of pixels
satisfy it. The IoU instead says which of those sets a reader is shown.

\textbf{Which classes SAM can explain at all depends on the seed.} Only seven of
the twelve class-and-seed cells have a baseline signal: Extremely\_Loud in all
three parents, Blip in two, Scattered\_Light and Violin\_Mode in one each. Even
Blip's baseline gap ranges from $0.029$ to $0.171$ across networks of equal
accuracy. Therefore the set of classes on which faithfulness can be assessed is a property
of the individual network, not of the class.

\subsection{Firing-rate distance and the disagreement subset}
\label{sec:disagreement}

The two measurements read the grid on the network's own terms 
(against the seed-to-seed reference of
Section~\ref{sec:hyperparameters}) rather than on the explanation's.

The Wasserstein-1 distance between the per-neuron firing-rate distributions of a
quantized model and its parent is below $0.015$ everywhere at 8 bits and reaches
$0.030$ at 4 bits. In both cases the values are under $0.022$ to $0.160$ 
that separates two
full-precision models differing only in seed. The 2-bit membrane arm reaches
$0.045$ at layer 3, which is still inside that band, while
 the 2-bit weight arms reach $0.148$
and $0.161$. Quantization down to 4 bits therefore moves the firing regime less
than a change of initialization does, which is the comparison Smith et
al.~\cite{smith2026} draw and the one in which the present grid looks
conservative.

The disagreement subset says the same of the decisions,as the Jaccard overlap
between the error sets of a quantized model and its parent is $0.78$--$0.80$ at
8 bits in the single-target arms and $0.63$--$0.70$ at 4 bits and at the 2-bit
membrane, against the $0.03$ in the collapsed arms. Of the $1\,435$ validation
images, between three and five are correct in the parent and wrong after
quantization at 8 and 4 bits, and six at the 2-bit membrane --- against $671$ in
the 2-bit weight arms. \textbf{The 2-bit membrane model makes the same decisions
as its parent, including the same errors, however explaining them with different
pixels.}

\textbf{The membrane arm does not save energy, infact it costs more.} Its weights stay
in full precision, so the cost of each operation never falls, and the
quantized membrane raises the firing rates and with them the number of
operations: $+1.5\,\%$ at 8 bits, $+4.4\,\%$ at 4, $+15.3\,\%$ at 2. The energy
follows, at $1.011$, $1.031$ and $1.108$ times the parent
(Table~\ref{tab:energy-grid}). What it does save is the state memory, 344\,KiB at
2 bits, almost a quarter of the total footprint.

Quantization raises the operation count in every arm, mildly at 8 and 4 bits
(1--8\,\%) and sharply at 2 bits, where it grows by half with quantized weights:
the collapsed networks, indeed, fire more. In the weight and joint arms the
saving still comes entirely from the cheaper operations.

\textbf{This grid did not find a trade-off between efficiency and explainability.} 
The configuration that changes the explanation at constant accuracy, the
2-bit membrane, is dominated on every axis by the 4-bit weight model: same
accuracy, IoU $0.739$ against $0.438$, energy $0.016$ against $1.108$ times the
parent, memory $512$ against $1\,176$\,KiB. The configuration to deploy is the
\textbf{joint 4-bit model}, as it features: accuracy at parity ($0.9835$), 
an explanation that
moves no further than continued training alone would move it (IoU $0.714$ against
a floor of $0.704$ and a fine-tuning level of $0.750$), estimated energy about
$1/60$ of the parent, and one eighth of its memory ($190$\,KiB).

One assumption determines the size of the saving. The first
convolution receives the analog image, and Table~\ref{tab:energy-grid} costs it
at the weight bit-width, which assumes the input is quantized to the same
width. At 4 bits that layer is 19\,\% of the joint model's energy, while if it is
instead costed in floating point, the joint 4-bit saving factor falls from 62 to
3.5. The operation counts and the memory column do not depend on the assumption,
and neither does the conclusion that the membrane arm is dominated, but the factor
by which the deployable model is cheaper.
