\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Quantization reduces the numerical precision of a neural network so that it can
be deployed under tight energy and memory budgets. A recent study on
convolutional networks showed that accuracy is not a sufficient criterion for
this operation: some architectures keep their accuracy at reduced precision
while the explanations of their decisions degrade substantially. This thesis
asks the spiking analogue of that question: \emph{does a quantized spiking
neural network base its decisions on the same evidence as its full-precision
counterpart, and at what energy cost?}

Spiking neural networks differ from convolutional ones in two ways that matter
here. They carry a membrane potential, a state variable that can be quantized
independently of the weights, and their explanations are sequences of maps
rather than single images. The working hypothesis is that quantizing the
membrane potential degrades the \emph{temporal} structure of an explanation
before quantizing the weights degrades its \emph{spatial} structure.

A four-block convolutional spiking network is trained on four classes of
transient noise from the LIGO Gravity Spy release, reaching
$0.9833 \pm 0.0014$ validation accuracy over three seeds under constraints that
keep the configuration hardware-realizable. Its decisions are explained with
the Spike Activation Map, a gradient-free method that reads the spike trains
directly; the free parameter of the method is fixed on the temporal axis it
governs, and its faithfulness is tested against a random baseline.

Before any quantized model is compared with its parent, the comparison metrics
are calibrated at three levels: the divergence produced by numerical noise, by a
thirtyfold change of the explainer, and by initialization alone. The third level
is far larger than expected. Two full-precision models differing only in random
seed, and agreeing to within one validation sample in accuracy, produce maps
whose Pearson correlation falls to $0.153$ on the worst class, against a
numerical floor of $0.975$. Spatial explanation-similarity metrics are therefore
valid only between a model and its own parent, while the temporal centre of mass
of the spike energy survives both comparisons. This calibration dictates the
design of the quantization study: every quantized model is fine-tuned from its
own full-precision parent and read against the numerical floor.

The study quantizes weights only, membrane potential only, and both together, at
8, 4 and 2 bits. A preliminary clipping study shows that the membrane potential
can be restricted to the unsigned range $[0, 2]$ at a cost of one validation
sample, which fixes the membrane grid.
