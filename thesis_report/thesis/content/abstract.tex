\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis asks whether a quantized spiking neural network (SNN) bases
its decisions on the same evidence as its full-precision parent.
The network is endowed with a second
quantization target,
the membrane potential, and explanations with a time axis.
A four-block convolutional spiking network with a shift-implementable decay and
eight time steps classifies four Gravity Spy gravitational-wave glitch classes at
$0.9833 \pm 0.0014$ validation accuracy over three seeds, and its decisions are explained by
the gradient-free Spike Activation Map (SAM). The comparison metrics are calibrated
first: two models differing only in seed produce maps correlating at $0.153$,
against $0.975$ under weight noise of one 8-bit step, so every quantized model
is fine-tuned from, and compared with the parent of its own seed. Weights,
membrane and both are quantized at 8, 4 and 2 bits. Down to 4 bits accuracy
holds, and explanations move about as far as fine-tuning alone. At 2
bits ternary weights collapse the classifier to $0.518$, while a 2-bit membrane
keeps $0.982$ and lowers the top-20\,\% overlap with the parent's explanation to
$0.438$, against a floor of $0.704$. The
predicted ordering, temporal before spatial, is not observed, yet within each
image the first-layer spike trains of every 2-bit-membrane model become exactly
periodic. The 2-bit membrane saves no energy, while a joint 4-bit model keeps both
at about $1/60$ of the estimated energy and one eighth of the
memory.
