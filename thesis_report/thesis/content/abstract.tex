\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Gravitational-wave detectors record a constant stream of short noise transients,
the glitches, which can mimic or mask astrophysical signals and impair results. 
Gravity Spy sorts
them by their time--frequency morphology, which is the first step towards tracing each
class of glitch to its instrumental cause.

This thesis builds a spiking neural network (SNN) for the classification 
task, with a membrane
decay realizable as a bit shift, and which classifies four Gravity Spy classes at
$0.9833 \pm 0.0014$ validation accuracy over three seeds. Deploying it
efficiently requires quantization, the core instrument of the thesis and its research question:
 whether a quantized SNN
bases its decisions on the same evidence as its full-precision counterpart, and
at what energy cost. In convolutional networks accuracy has been shown to survive
quantization while explanations instead do not: an SNN adds the membrane potential as a
second novel quantization target, whose explanations unfold in time.
The working
hypothesis is that quantizing the membrane disrupts the timing of an explanation
before quantizing the weights disrupts its spatial layout.

Explanations are computed with the Spike Activation Map (SAM), which is 
a gradient-free method
that reads the spike trains directly. The divergence metrics implemented
are first calibrated: maps of networks differing only in their seed correlate at $0.153$, so
each quantized model is compared only with the full-precision parent it is
fine-tuned from. Weights, membrane and both arms are quantized at 8, 4 and 2 bits each.

Results show that down to 4 bits accuracy and explanations hold. 
At 2 bits weights accuracy collapses to about $0.52$, 
while a 2-bit membrane keeps $0.982$ but
shares a top-20\,\% overlap of only $0.438$ with its parent, below the noise
floor of every class, on maps that remain faithful by the deletion metric. The
latter configuration saves no energy, while a joint 4-bit model keeps accuracy
with one eighth of the memory and an estimated energy between one sixtieth and
about one third of the parent's.

The predicted order is not observed, as the temporal metric of the explanation does
not move before the spatial ones. The membrane does act on timing,
in the dynamics: at 2 bits every first-layer neuron that fires at least
three times within an image does so at exactly regural intervals.
