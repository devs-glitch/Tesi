\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Quantization reduces the numerical precision of a neural network to make it
deployable under tight energy and memory budgets. A recent study on
convolutional networks showed that accuracy is not a sufficient criterion for
this operation: some architectures retain their accuracy at reduced precision
while the explanations of their decisions degrade substantially. This thesis
asks the spiking analogue of that question, and finds that it cannot be
answered until a prior one is settled.

Spiking neural networks carry a membrane potential, a state variable that is
quantized independently of the weights, and their explanations are sequences of
maps rather than single images. Measuring whether a quantized spiking network
reasons like its full-precision parent therefore requires metrics that resolve
both space and time. Before any such measurement can be interpreted, however,
one must know what those metrics report when nothing has changed.

This work builds and calibrates that instrument. A four-block convolutional
spiking network is trained on four classes of transient noise from the LIGO
Gravity Spy release, reaching $0.9833 \pm 0.0014$ validation accuracy over three
seeds under constraints that keep the configuration hardware-realizable. The
Spike Activation Map is implemented, its free parameter is fixed on the temporal
axis it governs, and its faithfulness is tested against a random baseline. Three
calibration levels are then established for the divergence metrics: the
divergence produced by numerical noise, by a thirtyfold change in the explainer
itself, and by initialization alone.

The central finding is that the third level is far larger than expected. Two
full-precision models differing only in random seed, and agreeing to within one
validation sample in accuracy, produce Spike Activation Maps whose Pearson
correlation falls to $0.153$ on the worst class, against a numerical floor of
$0.975$. Spatial explanation-similarity metrics therefore cannot distinguish a
quantization effect from initialization variance in this architecture, while the
temporal centre of mass of the spike energy survives the same comparison. Since
the literature reports values around $0.85$ as evidence of preserved
explanations without establishing either floor, such values fall between the two
and are not interpretable on their own.

The calibration is inexpensive, requiring no training runs beyond the multi-seed
baseline that is good practice regardless, and is proposed as a necessary
preliminary to any claim about explanation fidelity under compression. The
quantization grid it was built to support is specified in full and left as the
immediate continuation of this work.
