Glitch morphology

In the 0.5-second window (url1), the glitch occupies most of the visual space. This allows the CNN's convolutional filters to optimally extract the Blip's "teardrop shape," maximizing the visual signal-to-noise ratio.

Compatibility with transfer learning - 3 channel bottleneck
Famous neural networks like ResNet, VGG, or MobileNet were pre-trained on millions of standard images and expect a 3-channel (RGB) input.

If you combined four different images, you would end up with a 12-channel input.

To process that, you would have to alter the neural network's first layer, thereby destroying the pre-trained weights (which already know how to recognize edges, curves, and textures).

Since we are balancing the dataset by reducing the samples to approximately 2,000 per class, training a complex network from scratch on such limited data would almost certainly lead to overfitting. Using a single image allows you to leverage the power of Transfer Learning right out of the box. 3. Computational Complexity vs. Marginal Gain
To use four images, you could theoretically build four parallel neural networks that merge at the end (a multi-branch CNN). However, this quadruples the training time, RAM usage, and carbon footprint of your experiment. The literature shows that for the classes you have selected—which are visually very distinct at a local level—the tiny increase in accuracy provided by wider time windows does not justify the enormous computational cost.
